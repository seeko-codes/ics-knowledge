## ics-course/03-growth/01-briefing/02-right-vs-wrong-way-to-practice.md
- **stage**: 01-briefing, lesson 2/6 — establishes Kolb's, foundational to entire Growth phase
- **role**: lesson (5 sub-pages: Right vs wrong way, Practice guidelines, Stages of learning, Recommended tasks, Proper practice dissection quiz)
- **terms**:
  - Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle (ICS-modified) — 4 stages: Experience, Reflection, Abstraction, Experimentation; cyclical (experiment → next experience). ICS explicitly warns against learning this from other sources — their version differs from standard Kolb's
  - Guided Kolb's form — structured template tool (`/guided-kolbs`) for consistent cycles + easier feedback
  - Hierarchy of Competence — unconscious incompetence / conscious incompetence / conscious competence / unconscious competence
  - Marginal gains — progress-perspective framework; doesn't specify *how* to gain 1% (Kolb's fills that role)
  - Comfort zone / learning zone — regulatory feel-based concept for optimal practice pacing
- **claims**:
  - Practice ≠ automatically productive; only *correct* practice improves skill; incorrect practice wastes time + causes avoidable mistakes
  - Kolb's = "core pillar technique," one of most important skills in entire program
  - Kolb's = "engine of improvement"; marginal gains = "the road" — Kolb's operationalizes marginal gains
  - Confidence to experiment comes from trusting Kolb's (success guaranteed either via correct execution or valuable learning from failure)
  - Most gains at conscious-incompetence stage come from discovering more mistake types, even pre-first-success
  - Delay before reflecting loses detail + introduces confounding variables (e.g. reflecting on a whole week's studying conflates multiple separate "experiences")
  - Comfort-zone-expansion pacing is individual; must learn to *feel* the difference
  - Mindset-practice frequency depends on how often the underlying problem naturally occurs (≥1x/few days = rely on natural occurrence; <1x/few days = deliberately engineer exposure)
- **procedures**:
  1. Kolb's cycle: Experience → Reflection → Abstraction → Experimentation → (experiment becomes next Experience)
  2. Mindset-adoption Kolb's: (a) experience a challenge triggering the mindset; (b) reflect on what the mindset felt like; (c) abstract on approach taken — why it did/didn't work, how to improve; (d) experiment
  3. Pre-Kolb's mindset diagnostic: ask (1) what problem does this mindset address? (2) how often would I encounter it?
- **criteria**:
  - Kolb's frequency: end of every day (ideal) or ≥2x/week
  - Full cycle duration: ~30 min or less for most people (longer only for very complex issues); Zhao example explicitly caps effort at ~30 min/day even if incomplete
  - "Frequent" trigger = ≥1x/few days; "infrequent" = <1x/few days
  - Quiz: 92% (12/13) shown as passing
- **failure-modes** (Kolb's stage → common mistake):
  - Experience: outcome-used-as-experience (reflecting on outcome not process → hard to make productive adjustments)
  - Reflection: brief reflection (too short → insufficient info for abstraction); superficial reflection (long enough but not metacognitive)
  - Abstraction: theoretical abstraction (based on own unsupported ideas/bias, not the reflection); reflection-as-abstraction (just restates reflection, no analysis)
  - Experimentation: experiment overload (too many experiments/cycle); intention experimentation (vague "try harder," not specific/actionable); random experimentation (disconnected from abstraction findings)
  - Cycling: non-cyclical Kolb's (experiments don't carry forward as next cycle's experience)
- **examples** (quiz "Proper practice dissection," 92%/12-13):
  - Jeremiah: "I received 75% on my last maths test..." as Experience → demonstrates outcome-as-experience mistake; teaches Experience should be brief/concise, not necessarily a prior cycle's experiment
  - Zhao (x2 questions): non-linear note-taking during lectures, ~2 hrs spent, struggled with reflection → demonstrates brief/superficial reflection → theoretical abstraction → specific/actionable but futile experiment (root cause misdiagnosed); teaches ~30-min cap + self-awareness improves with time

## ics-course/03-growth/01-briefing/04-common-traps.md
- **stage**: 01-briefing, lesson 4/6
- **role**: lesson — 3 named traps + flashcard-overreliance case study
- **terms**:
  - Traps — behaviors causing disproportionate harm; even one can nullify otherwise-correct methods
  - Deathly spiral — technique helps short-term, harms long-term; deeper the fall, less time to escape; creates "learning debt"
  - Learning debt — future self must do extra work to pay for present inefficiencies
  - Bandwagon fallacy — using technique because it's common; common ≠ good; survivorship bias (failures less publicly vocal than successes)
  - Misfitting techniques — technique used incorrectly/wrong context; right technique = "short work"; high-effort-low-result signals misfit
- **claims**:
  - One trap can outweigh all correct techniques combined
  - Deathly-spiral archetype: insufficient skill-building time because too much time on ineffective "content-covering" methods → later forces fail-or-give-up choice
  - Judging technique effectiveness requires knowing BOTH who succeeded AND who failed with it
  - If method 1 needs less effort than method 2 for same result, method 1 is superior AND scales better with added effort (method 2 plateaus)
  - Flashcard overreliance = simultaneous case of all 3 traps
  - Flashcards themselves are useful (spaced retrieval, micro-learning) — overreliance is the problem, not the tool
- **procedures**: flashcard deathly-spiral mechanism (6-step causal chain): (1) system increases flashcard dependence → (2) creates learning debt (future repetition to cover material) → (3) struggles to build knowledge networks → poor memory/superficial understanding → (4) increases need for more repetition/gap-filling → (5) less time for other methods (or learning about them) → (6) daily card accumulation escalates cost to escape spiral
- **examples**: flashcard-overreliance walkthrough — concrete instance of deathly-spiral mechanism; carries unique 6-step detail
- **figures**: Traps%20are%20disproportionately%20harmful.jpeg (man stuck in pitfall); Deathly%20spirals.jpeg (short-term benefit vs long-term harm diagram); Bandwagon%20fallacy.jpeg (8/9 used method, 1 successful, yet 100% of successes used it); Effort%20vs%20method%20chart.jpeg (bar chart, method 1 vs method 2 effort-for-same-result)

## ics-course/03-growth/01-briefing/05-must-dos.md
- **stage**: 01-briefing, lesson 5/6 (5 sub-pages: overview, Prestudy, Revision, Focus, Techniques in school)
- **role**: lesson bundle — refresher/expansion of prestudy, revision(SIR), focus + school-context tactics
- **terms**:
  - Prestudy — pre-learning process creating a "reference outline" for the brain (jigsaw-puzzle analogy)
  - Chunking — grouping info into "chunks"/"sub-chunks" (term coined here for program-wide use)
  - Spaced repetition — weekly + monthly revision cadence, called "basic form"
  - Full recall — attempt recall (writing/drawing/mindmap/teaching/practice-Qs) before consulting source material
  - Time-blocking vs time-boxing — blocking = flexible scheduled time; boxing = hard stop at allotted time
  - Best-for-time outcome — goal of time-boxing: best achievable outcome in available time, not perfection
  - Parkinson's law — activity expands/contracts to fill available time
  - Pareto principle (80:20) — 80% of effect from 20% of input (examples: 80% learning from 20% info; 80% time wasted on 20% distractions; 80% of essay in 20% of time)
  - Eisenhower matrix — referenced only, not detailed, for unimportant-but-urgent tasks
- **claims**:
  - Meaningful (prestudied) info easier to remember than random info — brain recognizes importance, stores it more readily, raises retention/recall speed
  - Fixing prestudy early prevents/simplifies later downstream issues
  - Sleep has "dramatic carry-over effects" on focus/energy/mood via sleep-dependent memory consolidation
  - Frequent short breaks > infrequent long breaks (body recovers faster when less exhausted → longer total daily focus)
  - Mainstream teacher training lacks learning-science grounding → school-taught techniques can be outdated/anecdotal/detrimental; ICS advice should override conflicting school advice
  - Deliberately shallower prestudy before an unavoidable class can be used to induce useful engagement/cognitive load during otherwise-wasted class time; time saved redirected to interleaved retrieval
- **procedures**:
  1. Effective prestudy (6 principles): group info by relatedness; focus on big picture (details later); spend time on *why* something matters rather than memorizing/understanding it; create analogies/connect to prior knowledge; study entire topic (not individual classes) to preserve full relationship scope; aim shallow+broad over deep+narrow
  2. Basic revision (2 steps): schedule revision (weekly + monthly); use full recall before consulting notes/textbook
  3. Focus (3 pillars): sleep (8-9 hrs); remove distractions (distraction cheat sheet before session, remove listed items next session); frequent short timer-bound breaks (5/10/15-min intervals)
  4. School-context workarounds (4 options): discreetly use technique if undetected; minimum effort on unweighted homework via time-boxing; use class as dynamic quizzing/gap-finding environment; do homework during class if material already known
  5. Time-boxing procedure (4 steps): assign non-negotiable time for important tasks; schedule into day/week; allocate remaining time to unimportant/compulsory tasks; complete those as fast as possible within allotted time
  6. Last-resort classroom tactic: deliberately shallow prestudy before mandatory class → increases in-class engagement/load → redirect saved prestudy time to interleaved retrieval
- **criteria**: sleep ≥8-9 hrs; break sessions ~20-30 min study + 5-min break (vs poor 4hr study + 1hr break); break timer 5/10/15-min intervals; prestudy typically <30 min (principle flagged, detailed in file 06)
- **failure-modes**: teacher-enforced outdated/anecdotal techniques conflicting with ICS methods (remedy: follow ICS advice or use workaround tactics); overlong/over-comprehensive prestudy (implicit misfit, countered by last-resort shallow-prestudy tactic in forced-class context)

## ics-course/03-growth/02-technique-training/04-survive-and-thrive.md
- **stage**: 02-technique-training, lesson 4/10 — deepens lesson 3's network claim
- **role**: core mechanism lesson — 2-type relationship taxonomy + encoding-cycle framework; ends in scored quiz
- **terms**:
  - Analogous relationships — connections between new info and existing prior knowledge (methods: analogy-making, relating to known info, simplifying/renaming terminology)
  - In-context relationships — connections between new info and other new info (methods: immediate application, comparing new-new info, grouping into chunks, drawing directional arrows, spatial positioning by relatedness)
  - Bloom's (revised) taxonomy — classical hierarchy (memorizing/understanding = "lower-order," bottom levels); ICS states this hierarchy framing is criticized/"not entirely accurate"
  - Sung's practical encoding cycle — Dr Justin Sung's own framework (2023), non-linear, replaces linear Bloom's view; full explanation deferred to "lessons to come"
  - System 1/System 2 thinking — Kahneman's fast/reactive vs slow/deliberate systems (optional reading)
  - Hot-cold empathy gap — differing decisions under emotional/aroused ("hot") vs calm ("cold") states (optional reading)
- **claims**:
  - Info's value/memory-strength depends on relationship to other info; isolated facts/unconnected concepts forgotten regardless of whether "understood"
  - Both relationship types (analogous, in-context) should be pursued; many methods serve both
  - Zero relationships formable ⇒ material too detailed for current knowledge level → rebuild broader surface-level foundation via prestudy
  - Pre-learning problem-solving/practice questions "prime" the brain for relationship formation (both types)
  - Correct (big-picture) prestudy increases points of relevance for both relationship types
  - Memory & understanding are "symptoms" of good knowledge-structure creation, not achieved via direct intent to remember/understand
  - Authentic learning doesn't follow Bloom's linear bottom-up order; occurs via constant back-and-forth across levels
  - Brain seeks "energy-efficient shortcuts" in processing (System 1/2 framing) — ties to why unconnected info gets pruned
- **failure-modes**: inability to form ANY relationship w/ new material = diagnostic signal material exceeds current knowledge level; remedy = broader/shallower prestudy first
- **examples**:
  - Analogous (8 vignettes, each single-line, illustrative/classificatory only): Jill (muscle contraction~ladder-climbing), Geoffrey (dam electricity~wind turbines), Sarah (supply/demand~seesaw), Robert (genetics~Mendelian prior knowledge), Emily (trade/globalization~history), Samuel (20th-c literature~sociology), Ethan (chemical reactions~cooking), Alex (atom structure~solar system)

## ics-course/03-growth/02-technique-training/05-inquiry-based-learning.md
- **stage**: 02-technique-training, lesson 5/10 — introduces TLS, headline procedure of this block
- **role**: core technique lesson — inquiry-based-learning rationale + TLS 2-phase procedure; 2nd sub-page ("Doing TLS correctly") is truncated/near-empty
- **terms**:
  - Inquiry-based learning — learning by generating then answering self-created questions; changes what's consumed + how processed; ↑focus, ↑depth, ↑retention, ↑applicability
  - Traffic light system (TLS) — beginner inquiry-based-learning technique, 2 alternating phases (red/green), cycles until topic "mostly learned"
  - Red light — phase: generate keywords/concepts, then generate questions about them
  - Green light — phase: answer red-light questions via exploration, express findings in non-linear notes, periodically simplify/reorganize
- **claims**:
  - Studying only produces learning when consumed info has a purpose; questions create that purpose
  - Conventional learning is unfocused → too much info absorbed at once → harder processing → faster forgetting → wasted relearning; brain "doesn't like" unstructured info → feels tedious/unenjoyable
  - Application failure mainly caused by NOT applying immediately after learning, not by a general application-skill deficit
  - With correct inquiry, one can start from ANY keyword and still surface the truly most-important words/ideas (order-independence of correct inquiry)
  - Groups must emerge from critically-examined inquiry, not from original presentation order or preconceived (possibly suboptimal) organizational ideas
- **procedures**:
  1. TLS Red light: (a) list keywords/concepts covering main topic ideas (need not be comprehensive); (b) ask per keyword-set: (i) which keywords feel easier/more relevant now (start here); (ii) what am I curious about; (iii) why do I need to know this; (iv) what problems does it solve/how can I apply it; (c) capture any further emergent questions; (d) output = question list for green light
  2. TLS Green light (4 tips): (a) think of MULTIPLE possible answers per question (springboard, not single-answer lookup); (b) express discovered relationships in notes while exploring each keyword (expect early messiness); (c) after every 2-3 keywords, review/simplify/clean the growing map, judge what's worth keeping; (d) continually revise overall structure — map isn't "concrete" until much later
  3. Full cycle: green light ends when all red-light questions answered → return to red light → repeat until topic mostly learned
  4. Overflow-content handling: info too detailed/specific for question-answering → route to flashcards/other lower-order rote method (temporary parking, not primary method)

- **terms**:
