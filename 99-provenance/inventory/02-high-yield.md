## ics-course/02-high-yield/02-fundamentals/03-marginal-gains.md
- **stage**: Fundamentals, lesson 3/3 (final) — overview + 3 sublessons + recommended tasks
- **role**: lesson (framework)
- **terms**: Marginal gains — small compounding 1% improvements (cites James Clear's Atomic Habits); Stacking — ensuring gains compound positively not fluctuate/negative; Tracking — measuring/visualizing progress to steer growth accurately vs unreliable intuition; 3 gain types — Positive (compounds up, builds on prior, "no problems"), Fluctuating (1% changes don't build on each other → stagnation, caused by inconsistent progress-thinking), Negative (compounds down, caused by unreliable info/calibration/feedback); REDO framework — Reflect ("what might next 1% look like?"), Evaluate (assess improvement directions), Define (choose specific 1% gain + time estimate), Optimise (build specific plan); Positive motivation cycle / demotivation spiral — tracked progress sustains motivation, untracked progress → invisible gains → demotivation → burnout risk
- **claims**: consistency beats effort bursts; marginal gains more sustainable AND produce greater total effect than large strides; large jumps sacrifice "safety of success" for an unlikely boost; brain is bad at detecting small gradual change, must track actively; habit of viewing progress via marginal gains doesn't form naturally, requires deliberate retraining (habits operate fast/subconsciously, awareness alone insufficient)
- **procedures**: 5 stacking guidelines — 1. think about marginal gains consistently (default lens); 2. feed mistakes back as reflections for next cycle; 3. avoid random unstructured experimentation; 4. get regular feedback; 5. seek advice from reliable/qualified sources. REDO 4 steps as above. Recommended-task procedure — identify 1 marginal gain → attempt it → reflect → identify next gain via REDO → repeat twice more (3 full cycles to complete task)
- **criteria**: task-completion threshold = 3 full REDO cycles
- **failure-modes**: fluctuating gains (inconsistent progress-thinking → stagnation, demotivation, burnout); negative gains (bad info/calibration/feedback); learners apply marginal-gains thinking correctly to goal-setting but forget it when interpreting mistakes (old "I'm not improving" narrative persists)
