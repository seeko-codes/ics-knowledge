## ics-course/03-growth/06-base-camp/02-hipshot-bhs-variation.md
- **stage**: Base Camp, lesson 2/8. 3 sub-pages: intro, implementation, worked demonstration.
- **role**: lesson + worked-example (video-based demonstration, image artifact only).
- **terms**:
  - Hipshot — advanced BHS variation; rapid alternation of Aim and Shoot steps rather than separate passes; exploits trained high cognitive-load tolerance and rapid relational-network formation.
- **claims**: Hipshot works only with near-unconscious-competence of traditional Aim-Shoot cycling; attempting too early risks quality/accuracy loss (must revert to traditional BHS if struggling); Hipshot often emerges naturally as a byproduct of BHS mastery rather than being deliberately learned.
- **procedures**:
  - Hipshot practice procedure (6 steps): 1. Collect keywords as normal. 2. Perform Aim mentally to identify hypothetical logic layer. 3. Immediately Shoot on mental model to validate/correct. 4. Once satisfied with clarity, commit to GRINDE map. 5. Repeat from step 2 for each layer. 6. Perform Skin once initial map is satisfactory.
  - Standard BHS implementation (4 steps): Aim covers 1 week's material end of prior week (e.g., Sunday); Shoot basic/important concepts before main learning event if time allows (else morning-of); continue Aim-Shoot cycle during main learning event; Skin at end of day or end of week.
  - Accelerated BHS implementation (4 steps): 1 weekend Aim+Shoot ~60-70% of next topic's content for one subject; repeat weekly for different subject to stay weeks ahead across subjects; during week, Shoot remaining 30-40% before/during/after learning events; Skin at end of week.
  - Ultra-accelerated BHS implementation (3 steps): Aim+Shoot+Skin entire subject topic in one sitting on weekends/breaks; repeat weekly, accumulating weeks-ahead buffer per subject; continue spaced interleaved retrieval + gap-finding with remaining time.
- **criteria**:
  - Standard: Time efficiency 5/10, Difficulty Low.
  - Accelerated: Time efficiency 7/10, Difficulty Moderate; targets 60-70%/30-40% content split.
  - Ultra-accelerated: Time efficiency 10/10, Difficulty High; target ~90% coverage to 90% retention (not 100%); can complete a topic in ~1 day or less.
- **failure-modes**: attempting Hipshot without near-unconscious BHS competence -> reduced quality/accuracy; remedy: revert to traditional BHS.
- **examples**: Hipshot demonstration on VCE Unit 1&2 Accounting (Victorian AU curriculum), 1.5-hr session sped up; instructs viewer to compare Justin's chunking/relationship-building/Google-driven inquiry approach to their own — carries technique-comparison framing but no new procedural detail beyond what's already listed.
- **figures**: `ics-accounting-demonstration-scaled.jpg` — final GRINDE-style mind map from the accounting Hipshot demo session.
- **relations**: Hipshot replaces Aim+Shoot (per welcome page); requires traditional BHS mastery as prerequisite.

## ics-course/03-growth/06-base-camp/04-neuroticism.md
- **stage**: Base Camp, lesson 4/8. 6 sub-pages: Overview, Prevention, Planning, Measurement, Management, Chain Analysis.
- **role**: lesson set — multi-part framework across 5 stages (prevention/planning/execution-implicit/measurement/management) plus embedded self-assessment and diagnostic technique.
- **terms**:
  - Neuroticism — Big Five personality trait; associated with fixed mindset/fear of failure; beneficial in moderation (safety, standards, motivation), harmful in excess (anxiety, depression); explains high prevalence of anxiety/depression among high performers.
  - Catastrophising — psychological phenomenon where unprocessed thoughts make every outcome feel life-ending.
  - Cognitive distortions — mental biases/skewed thoughts increasing anxiety; common in high-neuroticism individuals; theorized ancient cautionary mechanism.
  - Avoidance behaviours — maladaptive over-avoidance of triggers leading to restricted, hypervigilant lifestyle; worsens anxiety.
  - Feedback Analysis Loop (FAL) — compares expectations vs. reality; mismatch signals process needs adjustment; framed as an application of Kolb's cycle; minimum required components: expectations, reality, comparison (extensible otherwise).
  - Focus training — see Base Camp 07/detailed here as antidote to fixation; conceptually: (1) put self in a low-distraction attention-drift situation, (2) practice letting go and returning attention.
  - Chain analysis — 6-step clinical-psychology technique for behaviour-change diagnosis (see procedures).
- **claims**: trigger avoidance reduces overwhelming neuroticism via 3 sub-mechanisms — awareness, attention, analysis; Kolb's cycle naturally improves emotional self-awareness/root-cause analysis; paying attention to emotion origin can reduce its negative impact (e.g., reframing anxiety as protective) breaking habit-forming anxiety cycles; forcing focus is less effective than training the brain to let go and re-center (attention as a trainable cognitive skill, not willpower); mind naturally wanders every 3-4 seconds even with trained focus — max ~10 sec "mental silence" even after years, but represents 3x baseline focus ability.
- **procedures**:
  - Distortion self-assessment: count matched thoughts from an 11-item checklist -> map to neuroticism tier.
  - Acceptance-development questionnaire (4 questions per identified distorted thought): frequency, triggers/timing, root cause ("why"), impact on mood/confidence/productivity — done on paper, may take days/weeks to fully answer, professional help flagged as sometimes necessary.
  - Reverse goal setting (5 steps): 1. Identify desired end outcome. 2. Define needed skills/attributes/mindsets for max certainty. 3. (Optional) rate needed level /10 each. 4. Rate current level /10 each. 5. Create improvement plan within deadline.
  - Top-down planning: start at end, work backward; best when goal is complex/multi-component or current skill level is hard to assess; feasibility check = does backward-traced "supposed" current state match true current state.
  - Bottom-up planning: start at current state, work forward; best when current skill level is clear or later-stage requirements are unclear; feasibility check = can step-wise projected development reach goal by deadline.
  - Focus training instructions (5 steps): 1. Sit comfortably, distraction-free, eyes closed. 2. Slow deep breaths. 3. Focus attention on breath sensations. 4. When mind wanders, acknowledge and release thought, return attention to breath (repeat). 5. Continue 15-20 minutes.
  - Chain analysis (6 steps): 1. Target behaviour (what to change). 2. Prompting event (specific trigger). 3. Vulnerability factors (what increased susceptibility — mood, prior events, habits). 4. Links (chain of actions/feelings/thoughts between prompt and behaviour). 5. Consequences (short + long term). 6. Solutions — 4 sub-categories: Exposure (limit exposure to prompt/vulnerability factors), Skills (develop/apply mitigating skills), Cognitive modifications (reframe meaning/impact), Contingency management (fallback plan if prior steps fail).
- **criteria**: distortion-count -> neuroticism tier table: 0-1 = low/high growth potential; 2-3 = some/moderate; 4-5 = high/significant limitation; 6+ = very high/highest-priority for immediate action. Focus training dose: 20 min/day x 30 days for significant improvement; 10 min/day shows effects in ICS experience; consistency emphasized over duration.
- **failure-modes**: avoidance behaviours (over-avoiding triggers) -> restricted/hypervigilant lifestyle, worsens anxiety — remedy: balance avoiding overwhelming triggers vs. managed exposure to unavoidable ones. Anxiety spiral: awareness of/focus on not-being-anxious worsens anxiety, can trigger panic attacks — remedy: acknowledge+appreciate the protective intent, reassure no real danger (cognitive modification). Past trauma + focus training: mindfulness meditation can resurface traumatic memories — remedy: do with therapist/psychologist guidance if trauma history known.
- **examples**: anxiety-gratitude reframe example (protective brain function); chain-analysis illustrative behaviours listed (procrastination, over-eating, social media, binge-watching, yelling) — generic, not a worked full example.
- **figures**: `Focus - when we struggle.jpeg` — graph of attention fluctuation and recovery dependent on ability to "let it go." `Focus - when we re-center easily.jpeg` — illustrates easier re-centering with trained focus.
- **relations**: Focus training here previewed, later formalized as core Base Camp technique (07/welcome page); FAL explicitly framed as an application of Kolb's cycle (prerequisite, taught in Growth phase Briefing per other files' cross-refs).
- **sophistication**: full/canonical — most clinically detailed self-regulation content in this chunk (5-stage framework + chain analysis technique).
- **scaffold**: heavy coaching voice throughout, self-assessment quiz-like checklist, recommended-tasks (mostly empty template bullets), "Dimensions: Base Camp" progress tags, warnings (trauma disclaimer), external reading (ACT, mindfulness research bibliography), app recommendations.

- **stage**: Base Camp, lesson 5/8. 4 sub-pages: Overview, Anatomy of a Habit, PEER-Peer Prep, PEER-Peer Easy/Exit/Reward/Peer. Note: "PEER-Peer summary" page present in frontmatter/heading only — no body content captured (effectively empty, not marked NOT CAPTURED but content absent).
- **role**: lesson — core habit-formation system, flagged "core pillar technique."
- **terms**:
  - PEER-Peer system — habit-creation system: Prep (cue optimisation), Easy (MVG), Exit (permission to quit at each MVG), Reward (locked/delayed reward), Peer (external accountability); flagged core pillar technique of the program.
  - Cue and response — habit anatomy: identify cues triggering response, remove bad cues, adopt good cues.
  - Conditioning — unrelated external stimulus evokes intended response (classical/Pavlovian conditioning applied to study behaviour).
  - Nudge theory — behavioural-science branch: environment shapes behaviour more than willpower; environmental-cue modification is a primary behaviour-change lever.
  - Minimum viable goal (MVG) — smallest actionable unit of a task, referenced as previously taught; common failure = setting MVG too difficult (remedy: as small as standing up/sitting at desk).
- **claims**: environment influences behaviour more than willpower (nudge theory); successful/productive people rely on nudge-theory-style environment design, burnout-prone/motivation-reliant people rely on willpower; cue optimisation is rarely ineffective if repeatedly iterated; PEER-Peer usage need decreases as behaviours automatize (Peer/accountability step becomes unnecessary once habit is fully automatic); conditioning studying with a subsequent enjoyable stimulus can make the brain enjoy studying itself, even without the reward.
- **procedures**:
  - Optimising cue and response (3 steps): 1. Identify cues triggering response. 2. Remove bad cues. 3. Adopt/integrate good cues.
  - PEER-Peer system application (5 components, not strictly ordered steps but named P-E-E-R-P): Prep (cue optimisation, iterate until effective), Easy (set MVG), Exit (permission to stop at each MVG), Reward (delayed/locked reward tied to goal attainment), Peer (external accountability, phased out once habit automatic).
- **criteria**: none quantitative beyond "reward should require minimum work, maximize delay before granting it."
- **failure-modes**: MVGs set too difficult -> procrastination persists (remedy: shrink MVG, even to "stand up"); relying on willpower alone to resist premature reward -> failure (remedy: physically lock reward away, enlist others).
- **examples**: bad cue = seeing social-media icon / seeing phone (remedy: delete icon, incognito mode, bury phone); good cue = books open to right page (arranged night before) / running shoes visible on doorknob. Conditioning example: pairing studying with an immediately-following enjoyable activity.

## ics-course/03-growth/06-base-camp/06-time-management-and-prioritisation.md
- **stage**: Base Camp, lesson 6/8. 3 sub-pages: Overview, Priority Myths, Priority 0-1 System (includes embedded FAQ).
- **role**: lesson — myth-correction + core prioritisation system + extensive Q&A.
- **terms**:
  - Priority 0 — core values and long-term vision; reviewed every 6 months.
  - Priority 1 — daily/weekly task prioritisation, driven by Priority 0 alignment.
  - Priority 0+1 system — intermediate time-management technique combining vision-level and task-level prioritisation.
  - Eisenhower Matrix (modified) — importance/urgency quadrant tool for task categorisation, used to determine priorities.
  - Urgency trapping — failure pattern where time is perpetually consumed by urgent tasks, preventing root-cause skill development; gap between challenges and skills widens over time.
  - Long-term vision vs. long-term goal — vision = open/abstract identity-level aspiration (not SMART); goal = specific target; visions preferred pre-age-30 to avoid tunnel vision from personality/priority change.
- **claims**: working faster reduces quality and increases errors (does not increase productivity) — brain is not a factory; trying harder without reflection prevents recognizing bad technique or correcting course; hard work can prevent smart work — achieving high performance without effort headroom for reflection requires accepting a short-term loss to enable working smarter; importance should be judged by consequence-of-failure, not felt urgency; scheduling urgent tasks first perpetuates urgency trapping — schedule important/non-urgent tasks first, fit urgent tasks around them; personality/priorities are near-certain to change, especially pre-age-30, so long-term visions (not narrow goals) should guide medium-term planning; deleted tasks that are truly important will resurface (self-correcting prioritisation heuristic).
- **procedures**:
  - Priority 0+1 system (4 steps): 1. Identify top priority via reverse goal setting. 2. Block time for top priority. 3. Fill remaining timetable by lower priorities. 4. On unforeseen events, shuffle lower-priority items to protect top priority.
  - Daily prioritisation routine: schedule/prioritise tasks the day before; on the day, capture emergent tasks into a list, feed into Eisenhower Matrix for next-day scheduling.
- **criteria**: Priority 0 (values/vision) reviewed every 6 months (stated twice, once as recommendation, once as explicit reminder); Priority 1 re-run daily or weekly depending on schedule volatility (busier people = daily).
- **failure-modes**: urgency trapping (common example: students fill time with urgent homework, never develop study skills; skill-challenge gap widens until goals become unreachable by the time it's felt as urgent) — remedy: schedule important-non-urgent tasks first (e.g., 20 min evening meditation), fit urgent tasks around them.
- **examples**: urgency-trapping example — students overloaded with urgent homework at expense of skill-building; meditation-scheduling example — 20 min nightly meditation block scheduled first, afternoon filled with other tasks; long-term-vision example — financial freedom + intellectually stimulating job + free time for hobbies + being patient/calm/confident/adaptable (illustrates why tutoring-only medium-term goals may not serve the vision).
- **tables**: none rendered as markdown table beyond descriptive Eisenhower Matrix image.
- **figures**: `Eisenhower Matrix (revised).jpeg` — modified Eisenhower Matrix showing quadrant priorities + per-quadrant instructions.
- **relations**: Priority 0+1 system replaces "Scheduling basics (prioritising guidelines)" per welcome page; builds on prior "Scheduling Basics" technique (continues, not fully replaced — timetable mechanics retained).
- **sophistication**: full/canonical treatment; extensive embedded FAQ deepens rather than simplifies (addresses edge cases: how often Priority 0/1 change, how to identify Priority 0, vision-vs-goal distinction, task-deletion confidence).

## ics-course/03-growth/06-base-camp/07-off-rest-timing.md
- **stage**: Base Camp, lesson 7/8. Single page (no sub-lesson split).
- **role**: lesson — replaces Pomodoro technique with a personalised work-rest system; includes research bibliography.
- **terms**:
  - Pomodoro technique — traditional fixed-interval work-rest method; critiqued as unoptimised (fixed 25-min focus / 5-min rest, non-research-derived origin).
  - OFF-Rest timing (Optimised-For-Focus rest timing) — personalised, adaptive work-rest system; aims to sustain efficiency/focus across max possible daily cycles.
  - Flowmodoro technique — externally-referenced variant closely resembling OFF-Rest timing's guidelines (cited, not detailed).
- **claims**: focus capacity varies situationally — cutting flow state short or extending unfocused time both suboptimal (fixed 25-min blocks mismatch this); fixed 5-min rest often insufficient — rest type/duration should match depletion state; resting at first sign of mental dulling (not at exhaustion) yields much faster refocus/restoration than waiting until noticeably tired — analogized to pushing a boulder while still rolling vs. restarting from a dead stop.
- **procedures**: Determining break type (3-step decision procedure): 1. If relatively fresh -> topical/miscellaneous break. 2. If low energy -> active relaxation break (focus training or mindful meditation suggested). 3. Regardless of type, time focus duration with a timer; set rest time to one-third of focused time. Optional: Wim-Hof breathing method for rapid re-energising if <10 min available.
- **criteria**: rest duration = exactly 1/3 of preceding focus duration (explicit ratio); rest should begin as soon as dulling starts, not at fatigue onset; Wim-Hof suggested specifically for <10-minute break windows.
- **failure-modes**: waiting until noticeably tired before resting -> much longer refocus/restore time, lower overall productivity (remedy: rest at first dulling signal).
- **figures**: `CleanShot 2023-10-21 at 18.28.29@2x.png` — image of a tomato-shaped kitchen timer, illustrating Pomodoro's non-research origin.
- **relations**: OFF-Rest timing explicitly replaces "Maintaining focus" technique (per welcome page) alongside Focus training; positioned as superior/personalised alternative to Pomodoro; references external Flowmodoro and "Rule of 52/17" as related but non-canonical variants.
- **sophistication**: full/canonical treatment — final new technique of Base Camp's technique set.
- **scaffold**: analogy (boulder-pushing), large academic bibliography block (mindfulness/meditation research, ~18 citations, mostly decorative support rather than load-bearing instruction), recommended practice task ("apply during ≥1 long study session, complete after ≥3 work cycles").
