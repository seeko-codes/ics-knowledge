## Statement

Behavior is driven by cues, environment, and vulnerability states, not by willpower or
intention. Durable behavior change comes from designing antecedents (cues, environment,
friction), managing vulnerability factors, and conditioning cue-response pairs with reward —
not from resolving to act differently.

## Core claims

- Anatomy of a habit: cue → response → reward conditioning; habits are built and dismantled at
  the cue and reward ends, not by attacking the response directly.
- Willpower-enhanced vs willpower-dependent design: systems that require ongoing willpower
  fail; systems that make the desired behavior the path of least resistance persist.
  Environment design (removing cues of competing behaviors, adding friction to distractions,
  pre-committing — "burnt ships") is the primary lever.
- V-ABC model: Vulnerability factors (sleep, stress, energy) → Antecedent → Behavior →
  Consequence; intervening on vulnerability and antecedent is more effective than direct
  behavioral effort.
- Behavior chains are analyzable: a target behavior is diagnosed by walking its chain (target
  behavior, prompting event, vulnerability factors, links, consequences, solutions per link —
  chain analysis).
- Starting friction dominates continuation: task initiation is disproportionately expensive;
  ramping devices (trivially small starts, open loops / Zeigarnik effect) convert initiation
  into momentum.
- Procrastination is not one mechanism: distinct drivers (aversive process, perfectionism-as-
  insecurity, decisional, pressure-dependence, generalised anxiety, attentional profiles) each
  respond to a different antecedent-level remedy.
- Conditioning transfers to cognition: focus, initiation rituals, and study-session structure
  are habit systems (PEER-Peer), and technique adoption itself relies on conditioned practice
  triggers (see L08).

## Independence

Without L10, all behavior guidance reduces to exhortation: habit systems, environment design,
procrastination remedies, and consistency mechanisms have no causal model. L08 describes the
skill trajectory; L10 explains what makes practice actually happen.
