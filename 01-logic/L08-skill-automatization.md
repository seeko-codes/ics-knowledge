## Statement

Learning techniques are themselves skills: they pass through competence stages toward
automaticity, consume cognitive budget until automatized, require far more practice than
theory, can only be acquired a few at a time, decay with disuse, and are actively interfered
with by the old habits they replace.

## Core claims

- Competence progresses unconscious incompetence → conscious incompetence → conscious
  competence → unconscious competence (elaborated in a 7-stage ladder with per-stage
  diagnostic signs). A skill is a "learning asset" only when it is understood, remembered,
  applied correctly, and performed consistently under pressure.
- Practice dominates theory: at least 5 hours of application per 1 hour of instruction (up to
  40:1 for complex skills); the perceived 75/25 theory-practice split is inverted in reality —
  most theory only becomes comprehensible through practice-discovered errors
  (theory-practice cycle).
- Parallel acquisition is capped: 2-3 techniques/processes at once; complexity compounds
  exponentially when more are introduced together (basis of staged curriculum and the
  scaffold-then-replace ladder — see technique-progression).
- Progression gates: ~80% consistency/competence on the current technique set before adding
  the next; moving early magnifies fundamental weaknesses downstream.
- Skills decline without use (technique decline), including silently in skilled practitioners
  who stop monitoring (complacency erosion).
- Old habits interfere: replacement requires unlearning; named anti-patterns are tug-of-war
  practice (alternating old and new methods under pressure) and Batman-style practice (using
  the new technique only when conditions are easy).
- Automatization frees load (L05): what is automatic no longer competes for working memory,
  which is what makes stacking techniques (e.g. Aim+Shoot+Skin into one system) possible.

## Independence

Without L08, techniques are information rather than skills: the ratios, gates, staging,
scaffolded-replacement ladder, technique decline, and unlearning machinery — the entire
program-sequencing logic — have no basis. L07 governs declarative memory over time; L08
governs procedural capability over practice.
