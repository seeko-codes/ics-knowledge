## Statement

Working memory is a hard, limited budget. Learning quality collapses outside an optimal load
band: overload prevents encoding, underload prevents engagement (both are real and symmetric —
the Goldilocks zone). Effective learning actively manages load: deliberately raising it through
higher-order engagement, then offloading it into external structure.

## Core claims

- Cognitive load is finite; when the brain must simultaneously resolve too many open problems
  (what does this mean, why know it, how does it fit, must it be memorised…), focus and
  encoding both degrade.
- Active management of cognitive load: higher-order thinking deliberately raises load; chunking
  and non-linear notes offload it into external structure, freeing budget for the next round
  (documented as a rise-and-offload cycle, figure: "Active management of cognitive load").
- Scaffolding/layering keeps load in-band: building logic before concepts before details means
  each pass carries a manageable increment (see layers-of-learning, prestudy).
- Underload is a failure mode too, not just overload: insufficient challenge produces
  disengagement and no encoding (LC48's symmetric Goldilocks framing).
- Load tolerance is trainable and state-dependent: fatigue during higher-order work is
  expected and partly diagnostic (see L09); rest restores budget (OFF-rest timing: rest ≈ ⅓ of
  focus duration, triggered at first dulling, not exhaustion).
- Techniques themselves consume budget until automatized (see L08): hence limits on parallel
  technique acquisition (2-3 at once) and staged introduction of complexity.
- External structure (notes, maps, second-brain systems) is legitimate cognition, not a crutch:
  offloading is what makes further higher-order processing possible.

## Independence

Without L05, there is no resource constraint in the system: no reason to scaffold, layer,
offload, rest, or limit parallel skills — and no explanation for overwhelm, the corpus's most
common reported failure state.
