## Statement

Finite resources — attention, study time, map space, practice effort — are allocated by an
explicit judgment of importance or leverage, never by presented order, urgency, momentum, or
comfort. Importance evaluation is a distinct cognitive act (the "evaluate" level): comparing
alternatives and judging their value against the big picture.

## Core claims

- For information: importance = contribution to the big-picture purpose ("why is this
  important?" answered with multiple relationships, then judged). Chunk structures, layer
  assignments, and note space are allocated by this judgment.
- For tasks: importance = the consequence of failure is unacceptable and unmanageable. This is
  explicitly decoupled from urgency; urgency-driven allocation ("urgency trapping") is the
  canonical failure.
- For skills/improvement: allocation goes to the current rate limiter — the constraint whose
  relief yields the largest system-wide return (bucket-with-a-hole model).
- Relevance is constructed, not intrinsic: how important a detail seems depends on whether a
  structure exists to give it relevance (see L02, layers-of-learning). Improving the top
  layers converts arbitrary details into relevant ones.
- Under overload (e.g. ultra-high-volume exams), allocation is triaged by confidence tiers
  (core > extended > examples > fine details) — accepting deliberate gaps is part of the
  mechanism, not a failure of it.
- Evaluation is comparative by nature: judging one item's importance requires candidate
  alternatives (multiple possible reasons, multiple candidate structures).

## Independence

Without L03, there is no selection criterion anywhere in the system: chunking loses
"importance-based", layers lose their ordering, time management loses the urgency/importance
distinction, and improvement loses rate-limiter targeting. L01 says deeper is better; it does
not say what deserves the resources.
