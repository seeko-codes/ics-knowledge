## Statement

Memory and understanding are functions of the cognitive operations performed on information,
not of exposure to it. Relational operations (comparing, contrasting, connecting to prior
knowledge), evaluative operations (judging value and importance among alternatives), and
generative operations (producing answers, structures, or explanations rather than receiving
them) encode more strongly and usably than receptive or repetitive operations (rereading,
verbatim copying, recognition, isolated memorisation).

## Core claims

- Processing exists on a spectrum from shallow (recognition, repetition, isolated
  understanding) to deep (analysis, evaluation, creation). The stimuli matter less than the
  cognitive processes they trigger.
- Higher-order learning = forming relationships between facts and concepts into a network, by
  (a) comparing information to find similarities and differences and (b) judging the importance
  of those similarities and differences. Lower-order learning is the absence of this — driven
  by habits of repetition, memorisation, and isolated understanding.
- Higher-order operations produce lower-order outcomes as a side effect (definitions and facts
  are retained in passing); the reverse is not true.
- Identifying similarities (analysis) is not the same act as evaluating the quality/importance
  of those similarities (evaluation); the evaluate level is where chunk-structure decisions
  live.
- Generation: attempting to produce an answer or structure — even unsuccessfully — is itself
  the encoding event ("the learning occurs when we attempt to answer").
- Depth of processing is trainable but habit-bound: cognitive habits, not conscious intent,
  determine the default processing depth (see L08 for the trajectory, L09 for calibration).
- Individual baseline differences exist ("deep processing tanking": high-baseline learners
  perform well despite mediocre technique, which confounds technique imitation across
  learners).
- The classical linear reading of Bloom's taxonomy (master lower levels before higher) is
  explicitly rejected; the corpus's replacement ("Sung's practical encoding cycle") treats the
  levels as interleaved operations within one encoding pass.

## Independence

Without L01, there is no account of why rereading and highlighting fail while comparing,
judging, and producing succeed — every technique in the corpus reduces to unexplained ritual.
