## Statement

Self-generated questions and problems are the steering interface of cognition: they direct
attention to what is relevant, prime the brain to seek specific relationship types, and create
the purpose without which consumption does not encode. The form of the question determines
which relationships get explored and therefore what schema gets built.

## Core claims

- Consumption encodes only when the information has a purpose; self-created problems create
  that purpose (studying only produces learning when the information consumed has a
  purpose).
- Inquiry focuses processing: with a driving question, less of the brain's effort is spent
  deciding what is relevant (attention is pre-committed), and the answer-seeking act is itself
  generative (see L01).
- The question-answer cycle is self-feeding: answering produces material for further, deeper
  questions (curiosity satiation loop).
- Question form maps to relationship types explored: "why is this important?" forces
  multi-concept evaluation against the big picture (highest yield); "how does X relate to Y?"
  probes specific relations (medium, 2-3 concepts); "what is / how does X occur?" yields
  isolated process/fact learning (low, risks pure memorisation). Questions one already knows
  the answer to are not inquiry (retrospective rationalisation posing as inquiry is a named
  validity failure).
- Emotionally engaging, genuinely curious questions bind more strongly than neutral ones.
- Not every question needs a full answer: complex questions do their encoding work in the
  attempt (generation), and unanswerable-now questions can be parked for lower-order methods.

## Independence

Without L04, there is no account of how processing gets initiated and steered: L01 says what
operations are valuable and L03 says what deserves resources, but neither says that
self-generated problems are the mechanism that points cognition and creates encodable purpose.
Aim, TLS, prestudy questioning, and question-quality criteria would be inexpressible.
