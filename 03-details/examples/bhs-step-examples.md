This is the honest inventory of what survives as text versus what is video-only across the
Aim/Shoot/Skin worked-example lesson sets. Across all three sets (26 subject sublessons total:
12 Aim + 8 Shoot + 6 Skin), only two subject-specific sublessons carry full worked technique
content in text (both under Aim: *Deep dive: Anatomy* and *Chunking for medicine*), plus one
general-technique sublesson (*Demonstration of core techniques 2*, also under Aim) and one
concept-overview sublesson (*Shoot Examples Overview*). **The Skin step has zero surviving worked
examples of any kind in text.** This is the single largest video-content hole in the Bear Hunter
System's documentation.

## Aim examples (ics-course/03-growth/03-ascent-i/06-aim-examples.md) — 12 sublessons

### Survives as text

- **Demonstration of core techniques 2** — uses ICS's own interactive curriculum module as
  practice content; instructs the learner to attempt the module before watching, to get an
  accurate self-assessment. Covers prechunking rules only, with no subject-specific technique
  detail: prechunking (forming chunks before inquiry) is sometimes acceptable and saves time, but
  only if the chunks remain hypotheses and continue to be modified; avoid prechunking if there is
  any difficulty engaging order control (breaking from how the content is presented); avoid
  prechunking entirely if spiderwebbing appears or the Aim step feels ineffective. "What matters
  is that chunks are formed independently and challenged with good inquiry."
- **Deep dive: Anatomy** — the fullest worked content in this set. Four candidate chunk-structure
  types for anatomy, usually needed in combination even within the same region: anatomical
  division (e.g. anterior vs. posterior), functional division (e.g. flexion vs. contraction),
  embryological rationale (can be highly effective when kept simple), and clinical
  application — itself subdivisible into surgical approaches/techniques, forming differential
  diagnoses, urgency of management, mechanism of injury/disease, and management complications
  (flagged as high-yield and "unfortunately, many medical schools do not prioritise this").
  "Fluency" in this context means identifying multiple possible chunk structures and evaluating
  which feels most intuitive and reduces rote memorisation. Chunk structures must be held in mind
  from the start of studying, not retrofitted afterward. Framing-bias red flag: many students can
  only think of one way to chunk anatomical information, despite clinical practice retrieving the
  same information in multiple ways. Interleaving is flagged as "especially" essential for
  anatomy specifically, more strongly than the general SIR guidance elsewhere.
- **Chunking for medicine** — the full worked 3-iteration retinal-detachment example (the
  corpus's canonical illustration of waterfalling, spiderwebbing, and framing bias together).
  **Content overlap note**: this example is also the canonical chunking-mechanism worked example
  assigned to W3's chunking-worked-examples file; full mechanism detail (all three iterations,
  figures Mindmap flow 1_0/2_0/3_0.jpeg) is documented there. Summary here: Iteration 1 = the
  common/obvious chunking, with everything funnelling through "Retinal detachment" → waterfalling
  (no lateral relationships, forgettable, forces memorising terminal points; "many other diseases"
  share this structure). Iteration 2 = lateral relationships added (pink = a newly-discovered
  chunk missed originally) → spiderwebbing (disorganised); evaluating connection density surfaces
  "Types" as a high-connection node worth re-chunking around. Iteration 3 = re-chunked around
  "Types" — resolves both waterfalling and spiderwebbing, retains all iteration-2 relationships
  but embeds them in the organisational structure itself, making the topic more memorable.

## Shoot examples (ics-course/03-growth/04-ascent-ii/04-shoot-examples.md) — 8 sublessons

### Survives as text

- **Shoot Examples Overview** — the only sublesson with substantive text. Introduces Aim-Shoot
  cycling: once proficiency with Aim and Shoot as individual steps exists, transitioning between
  Aim and Shoot seamlessly in a continuous cycle can begin (and may already occur without
  explicit instruction). Frames all subject-specific sublessons as demonstrations of this cycling
  "in action." No step-by-step cycling procedure is given beyond continued practice, after which
  the ability to cycle should develop naturally as a result of upper conscious competence in each
  individual step.
