## Rationale: limitations of the Pomodoro technique

The Pomodoro technique (fixed 25-minute focus blocks / 5-minute rest blocks) originated from a literal tomato-shaped kitchen timer, not from research. Two disadvantages motivate a replacement:

1. **Unoptimised focus time**: focus capacity varies by situation. A fixed block cuts off precious focus time prematurely when a learner is in flow, or forces sitting through a full block when focus has already lapsed well before time is up.
2. **Unoptimised rest time**: the technique does not specify how to use the rest period, and a fixed 5 minutes may be insufficient regardless of how much focus was actually expended beforehand.

## OFF-rest timing

**OFF** = Optimised-For-Focus rest timing. The aim is to keep efficiency and focus as high as possible across as many work-rest cycles as a day allows, by personalising the ratio and content of rest rather than fixing it.

Used for extended work periods involving multiple work-rest cycles. The type of rest taken varies with circumstances, but the aim throughout is to make rest itself as productive as possible, not merely a pause.

### Procedure: determining the type of break

1. If relatively fresh, take a **topical or miscellaneous break** (a break unrelated to active recovery — e.g., a change of subject or a casual activity).
2. If low on energy, take an **active-relaxation break** — focus training or mindful meditation is suggested (see `focus-training.md`).
3. Regardless of break type: use a timer to measure how long focus was actually sustained, and set rest time to **exactly one-third of that focused duration**.

### Rest trigger

Rest should begin **as soon as the brain starts to become dull**, not once noticeably tired. Resting at the first sign of dulling allows much faster refocusing and restoration; waiting until clearly tired before resting makes refocusing take much longer and reduces overall productivity.

**Analogy**: moving a heavy boulder costs very little energy to keep rolling if pushed a little whenever it slows — but if allowed to stop completely, restarting it costs far more energy. The same logic applies to focus: intervene at the first slowdown, not after a full stop.

### Short-break option

For breaks under 10 minutes, the Wim-Hof breathing method is suggested for rapid re-energising.

## Non-canonical variants

Flowmodoro and the "Rule of 52/17" are cited as related techniques that closely resemble the OFF-rest guidelines but are not part of the canonical technique — they are noted as external reference points, not taught in full.

## Bibliography note

The source lesson attaches an extensive academic bibliography (~18 citations) on mindfulness meditation and work-rest timing (e.g., Beauchemin, Hutchins & Patterson 2008; Cahn & Polich 2006; Zeidan, Johnson, Diamond, David & Goolkasian 2010). These citations function as decorative/supporting background for the mindfulness-meditation component of active-relaxation breaks rather than as load-bearing instructions for the procedure itself — the procedure above is fully specified without them.

## Competence target

At the Base Camp checkpoint, the target competency is dynamic, near-automatic adjustment of both rest timing and rest-activity type to optimise productivity — applying the procedure without deliberate calculation.

## Practice recommendation

Apply the method during at least one long, intensive focus session; treat as practised only after at least three full work cycles under the method.
