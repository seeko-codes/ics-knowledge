## Overview

MR FIG is a five-part exam-day optimisation for attention to detail and psychological state, applied on top
of an already-strong learning system. It sits alongside the "silly mistake" diagnosis (silly-mistakes root
causes: low attention to detail, or lacking logic — see ../03-details/tables/silly-mistakes-diagnosis.md):
where attention to detail specifically suffers under pressure, confidence, and self-belief, MR FIG is the
stated remedy set.

## The five components

1. **Mirror calibration** — positive self-affirmation: think and speak good things about one's preparation.
2. **Ritual** — create a sequence of events that conditions the learner to reach their optimal state.
3. **Focus training** — train the brain to focus using mindfulness meditation.
4. **Image training** — imagine performing well, including the specific steps taken to get there.
5. **Graduated exposure** — gradually simulate exam conditions during revision.

Each sub-technique carries its own external citation trail in the source (self-affirmation research,
ritualisation research, meditation research referenced to prior modules, image-training / mental-visualisation
research, and graduated-exposure / cognitive-behavioural research) — these are supporting evidence citations
for each component individually, not a single unified study of MR FIG as a combined protocol.

## Relation to broader exam-day guidance

MR FIG overlaps functionally with, but is distinct from, the standing exam-technique checklist
(exam-execution): the checklist's slow-breathing and graduated-exposure-therapy guidance for exam nervousness
and MR FIG's "Graduated exposure" and "Focus training" components describe the same underlying mechanisms
(parasympathetic activation, incremental exposure to pressure conditions) applied to two adjacent problems —
general exam anxiety versus attention-to-detail failure specifically. Focus training, mindfulness meditation,
and Wim Hof breathing cycles are also named generally as ways to create alertness and calm before a
high-stakes assessment, and specifically recommended for recovering focus and energy between back-to-back
exams.
