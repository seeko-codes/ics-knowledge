## Scope condition

This protocol is offered for anyone who struggles with focus and distractibility, including without a formal diagnosis. It is explicitly a set of *adjustments layered on top of* the standard course techniques (focus training, Kolb's cycle, and others), not a separate replacement system.

**Specialist-deference disclaimer**: these recommendations are based on program experience and non-specialist expertise around ADHD and ASD. Where a specialist's personalised recommendations contradict these adjustments, the specialist's advice takes precedence.

## The nine adjustments

1. Stabilise on relevant medications, as recommended by a medical specialist.
2. Practise mindfulness meditation — tangible benefits typically take about **two months** to appear. Walking or active meditation is an easier entry point than seated/resting meditation for those who struggle with stillness; carrying a small notebook to jot down intrusive thoughts during practice is recommended.
3. Use physical cues for ultra-short-term intentions and goals, checked via analogue timers.
4. Practise only **one technique at a time** (see exception below).
5. Keep a daily progress book; strictly limit any experiments or adjustments to **two points per day**.
6. Selectively mute Discord (or equivalent community/notification) channels to reduce ambient interruption.
7. Schedule Kolb's cycle daily — preferably immediately after medication, if there is a post-medication focus window.
8. Limit each Kolb's cycle to **30 minutes maximum**, enforced with an analogue timer; stop when time is up regardless of completion state.
9. Be patient with the process.

## Footnoted exceptions

- **On mindfulness/CBT effectiveness** (footnote to step 2): in some less common conditions, mindfulness meditation and cognitive behavioural therapy may be less effective — for example, there is ongoing research into the utility of CBT approaches specifically in early-childhood-trauma populations. Consulting a trained psychologist or psychotherapist to find a suitable approach is recommended where this applies.
- **On "one technique at a time"** (footnote to step 4): some techniques develop passively over time and do not require active focus during other activities — e.g., scheduling, which needs weeks to pass before feedback on its effectiveness is available. In these cases, working on multiple such techniques simultaneously is acceptable. The general guide is a sense of **system stability**: if there is a felt need to concentrate on applying each new technique individually, the system is already overloaded — new techniques should be turned into habit before more load is added.
