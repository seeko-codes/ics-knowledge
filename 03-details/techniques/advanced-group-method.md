## What it is

A composite technique combining generated questions, peer/group discussion, and practice
questions. It is an involved and extensive retrieval technique, but is rated among the most
consistently effective and useful across all declarative-focused subjects — the highest-rated
declarative technique in the interleaving table, appearing at mid-order and higher-order alike
(marked with an asterisk for multi-order suitability).

## Instructions (practice instructions)

1. Choose a topic.
2. Create a study group with at least 1 other person of a similar knowledge level (or higher).
3. Each person creates a practice exam. Cover some mid-order questions as well as more challenging
   and nuanced higher-order questions; address strictly lower-order questions through other
   retrieval methods instead.
4. Each participant creates a perfect answer sheet for their own exam: first brain dump to the
   best of their ability, then review learning materials to refine and perfect the answers.
5. Participants swap practice exams with each other, each holding onto their own answer sheet.
6. Each participant answers the exam received from memory, to the best of their ability.
7. Mark answers where uncertainty was felt, for further review.
8. Each participant creates a perfect answer sheet for the exam just taken, by reviewing learning
   materials.
9. Participants swap answer sheets with each other.
10. Compare answers against one's own answer sheet and against the other's. Look for differences
    between the two answer sheets.
11. If there are any points of disagreement or areas to explore further, discuss them together to
    reach a conclusion.

## Why it works

This method tests multiple orders of learning from multiple perspectives and forces positive
generative effects of learning at steps 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, and 10. For each hour of time spent, the
amount of learning consolidation and gap-testing achieved is one of the highest of all retrieval
methods.

## Practical notes

As this method is time-consuming, it is recommended to split the process across multiple days.

> Due to its high effectiveness **and** efficiency, the advanced group method for practice
> questions is generally recommended for all learners to try and use, where possible.

## Substitutability

The advanced group method substantially overlaps with test-target-teach (see
03-details/techniques/test-target-teach.md), which is offered as an interim/shortcut framework for
learners who have not yet mastered the full interleaving table. Learners already confident with the
advanced group method can use it directly in place of test-target-teach.
