## Core reframe: "not a battery"

Energy is not modeled as a simple linear battery where time spent equals energy lost. Two claims follow from this:

- **Alignment generates energy**: activity that is aligned with a person's own goals and values can generate energy rather than only cost it — this claim is made independently in both source clinics (LC54's "battery" reframe and LC65's alignment/energy discussion), which corroborate each other.
- **Effect is controllable only indirectly**: the amount of effort put into a supplementary area (hobbies, rest, sleep, meditation) is controllable directly, but how much effect that effort has is not — and the type and magnitude of the effect varies over time and across situations. This is why a fixed formula for how much rest "should" restore energy does not hold in practice.

An observed asymmetry: inefficient/passive processes (e.g., passive learning) take *longer* to recover from than active processes, despite draining more energy in the moment — recovery time is not simply proportional to how draining an activity felt while doing it.

## The right approach: two parts

1. **Awareness** — understanding how a given activity actually affects the individual (not assuming it based on how it affects people generally).
2. **Active monitoring/regulation** — continuing to track that effect over time so it can be regulated deliberately, rather than assessed once and assumed stable.

## Three-step framework

1. **Identify processes with a non-negotiable threshold** and protect time for them (e.g., a fixed minimum of sleep or a specific recovery activity that cannot be skipped without cost).
2. **Identify the minimum time needed** to invest in improving the efficiency of learning/work, sufficient to make meaningful progress.
3. **Use the remaining time** to work toward the desired output (learning or work itself).

Arriving at the right allocation under this framework is described as requiring iteration — repeated Kolb's cycles, monitoring, and experimentation to make the framework's application relevant to a learner's current, changing situation, rather than a value that can be set once.

## Sleep parameter

A single, continuous, uninterrupted sleep of roughly **8 hours** at night is treated as the ideal baseline.

## Rest-type selection (link to OFF-rest)

The same "match the recovery activity to the actual depleted state, not to a fixed rule" logic underlies OFF-rest timing's break-type selection (see `off-rest-timing.md`): a fresh state calls for a topical/miscellaneous break, while a low-energy state calls for an active-relaxation break such as focus training. Energy management operates at the scale of a day or week; OFF-rest timing applies the same state-matching principle within a single work session.

## Wellbeing prerequisites (distinguished from strategies)

Prerequisites are the baseline conditions resilience is built on, distinct from active coping strategies applied on top of an already-depleted baseline. Breaks alone are described as insufficient for genuine burnout recovery — alignment with meaningful goals is required alongside rest, not instead of it. Activities cited for a disproportionate boost to energy and mental state relative to the time they cost: exercise, human connection, sun/outdoor exposure, and sleep. Mindfulness meditation is positioned as the longer-term resilience-building skill (implemented via focus training, see `focus-training.md`).
