## The cycle itself

Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle: **Experience → Reflection → Abstraction → Experimentation**,
cyclical (the experiment feeds forward as the next cycle's experience). This is described as a
"core pillar technique" — one of the most important skills in the entire program; many of the
program's own methods were themselves developed by applying research and reflecting on it through
thousands of Kolb's cycles.

## Common mistakes by stage

| Stage | Mistake | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Experience | Outcome used as experience | Reflecting on an outcome rather than a process makes it difficult to identify productive adjustments |
| Reflection | Brief reflection | Reflection too short leaves insufficient information for the abstraction step |
| Reflection | Superficial reflection | Sufficient length, but the content is not metacognitive enough to yield meaningful process insight |
| Abstraction | Theoretical abstraction | Based on the learner's own unsupported ideas (possibly biased) instead of analysing the reflection |
| Abstraction | Reflection as abstraction | The abstraction simply restates the reflection rather than analysing it |
| Experimentation | Experiment overload | Too many experiments attempted in one cycle |
| Experimentation | Intention experimentation | Experiments framed as vague effort ("try harder") rather than specific and actionable |
| Experimentation | Random experimentation | Experiments disconnected from what the abstraction actually found |
| Cycling | Non-cyclical Kolb's | Experiments do not carry forward to become the next cycle's experience |

## When and how often to use Kolb's

Kolb's can be used to develop any new skill; in the program it is used whenever anything new is
learned — after every sub-lesson, every lesson, or every 2–3 lessons — gaining some applied
experience with a technique, mindset shift, or tip before moving on.

Frequency: ideally at the end of every day; at minimum twice per week for most people, since doing
one cycle after every single experience is not viable. Waiting too long before reflecting loses
detail and introduces confounding variables — a single end-of-week cycle covering an entire week's
studying, for example, conflates many separate experiences into one.

## What to practise on

Any material works: already-learned material, material to be learned in the future (giving the
secondary benefit of studying ahead), or irrelevant material (Wikipedia articles, topics of
personal interest). For non-encoding skills such as time management, the technique is applied to
general life (scheduling the week, nightly routines); the exact application depends on which
technique is being practised.

## Practising mindset changes

Because outcomes from mindset changes are not always obvious or quantifiable, mindset adoption
still follows the Hierarchy of Competence, but with its own diagnostic framing. Pre-cycle
diagnostic (2 questions):
1. What problem does this mindset address?
2. How often would this kind of problem be encountered?

If the problem is encountered frequently (at least once every few days — e.g. stress, focus,
procrastination), enough natural practice opportunities will occur on their own. If infrequent
(less than once every few days), the problem should be deliberately engineered so practice
opportunities do not have to wait weeks or months.

Mindset-adoption cycle (4 steps): (1) experience a challenge or problem that induces the target
mindset; (2) reflect on what the mindset felt like during the experience; (3) abstract on the
approach taken to activate or maintain the right mindset — why did it or didn't it work, how could
it be done better; (4) experiment with the refined approach.

## Hierarchy of Competence

| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| Unconscious incompetence | No knowledge of the new skill; unaware of being bad at it |
| Conscious incompetence | Performs and reviews the skill incorrectly, but aware of not being good at it |
| Conscious competence | Can correctly analyse and apply the skill, but it requires active effort |
| Unconscious competence | Can perform the skill intuitively, with minimal thought and effort |

Kolb's is described as the "engine of improvement" against this ladder: it is what lets each
experience be converted into learning and each mistake into an adjustment. Confidence to
experiment comes from trusting the process — either the technique is executed correctly, or a
failed attempt still yields valuable learning. Full relation to marginal gains ("the road"):
../../02-concepts/kolbs-cycle.md.

## Tooling and comfort-zone calibration

A `/guided-kolbs` form is provided to help complete cycles consistently and make feedback on them
easier to give. The amount of experience needed before moving to a new lesson is individual;
learners must learn to recognise, by feel, the difference between the comfort zone and the
learning zone, and regulate practice to stay in the learning zone.
