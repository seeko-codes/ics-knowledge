## Definition

Reverse goal setting is any method that dissects a desired outcome into its determinants and traces them back to the current position, used to identify Priority 0/1 targets and to break down goals in the neuroticism-planning stage. An alternative method incorporating force-field analysis may be more appropriate for especially complex goals (referenced but not detailed in the source material).

## Procedure

1. Identify the desired end outcome.
2. Define the skills, attributes, and mindsets necessary to achieve this with maximal certainty.
3. (Optional) rate the required level for each, out of 10.
4. Rate the current level for each, out of 10.
5. Create a plan to improve the necessary skills and attributes within the desired deadline.

## Direction: top-down vs. bottom-up

Plans built from reverse goal setting can proceed in either direction, each with its own best-use case and its own feasibility check.

### Top-down

Start at the end and work backwards.

- **Best used when**: it is easier to deconstruct the goal from the end result (often true of complex goals with several components and varied timings); or it is difficult to assess current skill/attribute level, or development on them has not yet begun.
- **Feasibility check**: does the *true* current state match the *supposed* current state arrived at by tracing backward from the desired goal to the present day? If, working backward, the required current state turns out to be better than the actual current state, the plan is not feasible as designed and must be reconsidered.

### Bottom-up

Start at the current state and work forwards.

- **Best used when**: current skill/attribute level is relatively clear; it is unclear what will be needed at later stages of achieving the goal; or there is a more direct relationship between the skills/attributes and the goal.
- **Feasibility check**: can the goal be reached within the deadline based on the step-wise projected progression of skill and attribute development? If, based on the anticipated pace of development, the desired level cannot be reached in time, the plan is not feasible and must be reconsidered.

**Note**: a bottom-up approach still counts as reverse goal setting as long as the required skills and attributes are identified by analysing the desired goal itself — the direction of construction (forward from now vs. backward from the goal) does not change that the *content* of what to build is derived from the goal.

### Using both

In many cases, running both a top-down and a bottom-up analysis on the same goal helps surface gaps that either direction alone would miss, and cross-validates the plan's feasibility from two angles.
