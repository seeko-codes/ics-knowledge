## Overview

CPJ (Conclusion-Process-Journey) is a reframing technique that converts a mistake or an uncomfortably challenging experience into process information, rather than allowing it to become a negative self-judgment.

## Procedure

1. **Conclusion**: identify the false "therefore I am..." self-statement made about oneself following the event (e.g., "I failed, therefore I am not smart enough"). These conclusions are identified as damaging to psychological wellbeing and as blocking learning from the experience — they are treated explicitly as false, not as accurate self-assessment.
2. **Process**: identify the specific processes that actually led to the event — what was done, in what order, under what conditions — as distinct from a character judgment about the person who did them.
3. **Journey**: create a list of actionable improvements to try next time, derived from the process analysis in step 2.

## Application task

1. Try a new technique (any technique).
2. On making a mistake or feeling uncomfortably challenged, apply the CPJ framework to convert the experience into desirable learning, per the three steps above.

**Completion criterion**: the framework should be applied at least twice (or as many times as needed) until it starts to feel more comfortable to use.

## Reframe examples

- Instead of "(event), therefore I am (X)" — a fixed self-judgment — the reframe is "(event), now I will (reflect/adapt/become more insightful)," shifting from a conclusion about identity to a commitment about the next action.
- A specific instance of the Conclusion-level reframe: replacing "I'm just not good at this" with "I'm just not good at this **yet**," followed by "What can I learn from this instead?"

## Relation to locus of control

The Journey step operationalises the same locus-of-control principle underlying the golden rule of learning (see `mindset-emotion.md`): self-worth is not made contingent on the outcome or on the self-narrative attached to it; growth comes from focusing on the controllable process, which is exactly what the Process and Journey steps direct attention toward.
