## Definition

A skills audit is a structured review or assessment of a learning system, used to pinpoint issues
and drive targeted improvements. It works by reviewing current techniques against the guidelines
they were taught against, to identify gaps.

## When an audit is needed

1. **After rushing or selective learning.** Learning skills too quickly (without adequate
   practice) or skipping techniques significantly increases the chance of introducing technique
   mistakes or deficient systems, and these can be difficult to root-cause even for an experienced
   coach without a structured audit.
2. **Resuming after a long break.** A long break causes forgetting or loss of skill through
   disuse — **technique decline**. An audit finds the specific weak areas and produces an
   improvement plan.
3. **Progressing to new stages that assume mastery of prior stages** — e.g. moving from
   Fundamentals 2 to Briefing, or from Technique Training to Ascent 1, where later material builds
   directly on assumed foundational skills.
4. **Feeling stuck or lacking progress.** An audit surfaces specific, sometimes subtle, technique
   mistakes behind a plateau.

## Mindset for performing an audit

A growth mindset is treated as a precondition for an accurate audit: it is more important to
recognise being mistaken than to assume correctness. Keep an open mind (be willing to find areas
to work on even where significant time has already been invested); be honest (a "bad" rating is a
sign of commitment to continuous improvement, not a negative reflection on past effort); take the
time needed (an advanced-stage audit can take hours, and in some cases a skill must be applied and
practised further before it can be accurately self-assessed); keep it regular (roughly every 2–3
months, more often when learning new techniques quickly).

## LEEP procedure

**Step 1 — Learning flow map.** Build a flow map reviewing every step of the current learning
process (using the Gear Checklist from Briefing as a reference tool). Confirm the flow includes
techniques from every completed stage.

**Step 2 — Evaluate.** Check techniques against course guidelines: checkpoint videos, Milestone
Checklists, Meta-Checklists, stage objectives, and End-of-Stage Quizzes. Look for gaps or problems
from rushing or missing parts of a technique. Rate each technique **1–5** (1 = poor,
5 = excellent), being as objective and accurate as possible.

**Step 3 — Error correction.** Fix gaps by adjusting technique execution against target metrics
and stage checkpoints. Apply with a focus on consistency and practice, limiting error correction
to **1–2 aspects at a time** to prevent overload. Seek feedback via self-evaluation, the Discord
community, or Office Hours.

**Step 4 — Perfect practice.** Use Marginal Gains Tracking and Kolb's Cycle to develop the
identified skills further (see ../../02-concepts/marginal-gains.md and
../../02-concepts/kolbs-cycle.md), reflecting on mistakes through systematic experimentation with
both frameworks. Reflection here is cyclical — fixing a technique or mistake may take multiple
experiments, especially where rushing or technique decline caused the original gap. A gradual
approach with a growth-mindset framing is recommended when bridging these gaps.

## Rating thresholds

- **Skill screen threshold:** focus on areas scoring below **4/5**.
- **Comprehensive-audit threshold:** focus on areas below **4/5**, or below **5/5** for
  foundational techniques — a stricter bar for foundational skills specifically.

## Skill screen vs. comprehensive audit

| | Skill screen (faster) | Comprehensive audit (detailed) |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Review previous checkpoint videos and major lessons introducing new techniques | Review every lesson, checkpoint, and end-of-stage quiz from the beginning of the course |
| Rating | 1–5 per lesson, on competency, accuracy, and consistency | 1–5 per lesson, on competency, accuracy, and consistency |
| Focus | Identify the key aspects of techniques needing high competence | Look for discrepancies between theoretical understanding and practical application, alongside key aspects of technique |
| Threshold | Below 4/5 | Below 4/5, or below 5/5 for foundational techniques |

## Program touchpoints

The first canonical reference to a skills audit is at the Briefing stage; further references
exist at the Final Checkpoints of Ascent 2 and Base Camp. Audit instructions remain usable ahead
of reaching these stages.
