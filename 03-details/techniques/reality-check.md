## Overview

A 5-step procedure for closing the gap between an intended schedule and actual behaviour, used to surface invisible self-management barriers before attempting to address them.

## Procedure

1. **Make a schedule.**
2. **Track** actual time spent alongside the schedule.
3. **Compare** the schedule against the actual tracked time.
4. **Identify barriers** — be specific about what caused the deviation.
5. **Overcome barriers** through either *accepting* or *addressing* them.

## Accepting vs. addressing

- **Accepting**: there is nothing more that can reasonably be done to change this barrier.
- **Addressing**: making changes to eliminate or reduce the barrier's impact.

The choice between accepting and addressing a barrier is a decision about the level of discomfort one is willing to tolerate, not an objective difficulty judgment. Most people accept issues before sufficiently exploring whether they could actually be addressed — many "accepted" barriers are not truly impossible to address, only uncomfortable to address.

**Note**: not every barrier needs to be addressed. Where addressing a barrier would create disproportionate inconvenience or discomfort, it is reasonable to decide not to remove it — in which case the question shifts to whether the barrier's *impact* can at least be mitigated, even if the barrier itself remains.

## Completion criterion

The procedure is considered complete once run for at least **3 days** (or longer, at the learner's own pace, to continue surfacing hidden barriers and habits).
