## Overview

The Traffic Light System (TLS) trains inquiry-based learning through two alternating phases:
**red light** (generate questions, direct focus) and **green light** (satiate curiosity by
answering those questions). Red light forces question-generation and breaks the passive habit of
reading or consuming information without a clear big-picture purpose. Green light is when
curiosity is satiated by reading, listening, and searching through content to find relationships,
write notes, and think about how everything connects.

## Red light procedure

1. Create a list of keywords and concepts covering the topic. This list does not need to be
   comprehensive, but should try to cover the topic's main ideas.
2. Once the keyword list exists, ask the following questions of it:
   1. Which keywords feel easier or more relevant immediately? (Start with these.)
   2. What am I curious about?
   3. Why do I need to know this?
   4. What problems does this help solve, or how can it be applied?
3. Further questions that arise while asking the above — especially anything tied to genuine
   curiosity — are noted for the green light step.
4. Red light ends with a list of questions to answer.

## Green light procedure

Answer the red-light questions to learn the topic organically. The goal is not to read
everything and memorise all the information, but to naturally answer the generated questions.
Four governing principles:

1. **Think of multiple answers for each question.** The point is not to find one perfect answer
   but to use the question as a springboard to multiple relationships.
2. **Express relationships in notes as they are found.** Exploring each keyword surfaces many
   relationships between keywords; these should be expressed in notes even though the result will
   be messy at first.
3. **Simplify every 2-3 keywords.** After every two or three keywords, review the growing map,
   simplify it, and make it cleaner — judging what is important and worth keeping.
4. **Keep revising the overall structure.** The map is not "concrete" until much later; everything
   mapped at this stage exists only to help thinking and organise thoughts, not as a committed
   final structure.

Green light ends once all red-light questions have been answered.

Non-linear note-taking is required to hold the volume of relationships TLS surfaces; attempting
to track them mentally causes overload. Learners still using the earlier collecting-and-processing
technique are directed to move toward fully non-linear ("mindmap"-style) note-taking once using
TLS.

### Escape hatch for unanswerable detail

Some information is too detailed or specific to be reachable through question-answering. This
material is routed to flashcards or another lower-order rote-memorisation method for the time
being, rather than blocking progress through the red/green cycle.

## Cycling rule

Once green light is complete (all red-light questions answered), return to red light and repeat
the cycle until the topic feels mostly learned.

## Starting-keyword independence

Any keyword felt to be an important idea is a valid starting point. With correct inquiry, a
learner should be able to start from any keyword and still arrive at identifying the truly most
important words. The goal of inquiry is to compare and contrast ideas and see how concepts
influence and relate to one another, then group words based on that information — groups must not
be dictated by the order in which the material was presented, or by preconceived (possibly
suboptimal) ideas about how it should be organised. Effective learners arrive at their own,
critically examined conclusions about how to organise information.
