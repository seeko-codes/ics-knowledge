## Definition

Aim points the brain in the right direction by asking questions that force it to think in
specific patterns and form specific relationships. It is an intermediate-level inquiry-based-
learning skill that builds directly on the TLS red-light skill.

## The two core questions and their distinct functions

Two questions consistently create high-quality networks for any subject:

- **"Why is this important?"** Focuses attention on the big picture. It creates focus, purpose,
  and alignment between ideas, and pushes thinking to the *Evaluate* level — making judgments and
  prioritisations of networks. Asking this question critically makes chunks more memorable and
  intuitive.
- **"How is this related to ___?"** Explores additional relationships that may not be obvious.
  Exploring why something is important makes it difficult to escape existing biases and
  preconceptions about the information; two concepts may initially seem unrelated, and only when
  forced to explore their relationship does a new perspective or potential chunk emerge.
  Sometimes this forces re-evaluation of the entire chunk structure and the reasons for
  importance previously assigned.

Although the two questions may look conceptually similar on the surface, they are treated as
distinct — arrived at "after years of trial and observation" — precisely because each interrupts
a different blind spot: the first counters absence of evaluative judgment, the second counters
bias/preconception in what looks "obviously" unrelated.

## Aim's three purposes

1. To make new information relevant and immediately form networks.
2. To activate higher-order learning as early as possible.
3. To create chunks and relationships that are robust, versatile, and suited to retrieval needs.

## Meta-checklist (feeling -> cognitive outcome)

| How it can feel | Cognitive outcome |
|---|---|
| Overwhelming, from the number of relationships surfaced while answering "why is it important?" | Rapidly creates a network of possible relationships to refine. |
| Confusing, since many possible non-linear structures exist for the main concepts and deciding between them takes real effort | Adds a high degree of prioritisation and evaluation — one of the highest orders of learning. |
| Time-consuming, because each keyword or concept requires deep examination | The deep examination consolidates learning; this feeling fades as speed and efficiency increase. Over time the brain becomes better at creating networks, prioritising them, and deciding on the best hypothetical structure. |

(The TLS meta-checklist referenced as identical at this stage is documented in
../techniques/tls-procedure.md.)

## Prestudy-interaction rules

Prestudy and Aim often overlap but are not the same thing. Prestudy is a *chronological* step
(when learning happens); the Bear Hunter System is *how* learning happens. Aim should be one of
the very first steps — ideally the first — in any learning journey:

- If Aim happens before an intensive main learning event, it counts as prestudy.
- If there is no prestudy, Aim should still be the first step of the learning journey; because
  this kind of thinking is difficult to manage during an intensive lecture or class, Aim may have
  to move to a self-study session after the main event instead — this is not optimal.
- The amount covered during prestudy is a scope decision. A brief prestudy of only 5-10 minutes
  does not leave time for a full Aim; in this case only a superficial Aim is done, developing the
  largest chunks and major relationships only. A more comprehensive prestudy supports a full Aim
  pass, with more detail added; once the Shoot step (which follows Aim) has been learned, Shoot
  can also be folded into a sufficiently comprehensive prestudy.

Aim completely replaces TLS red light: red light creates some initial connections and
relationships, but Aim is a more focused and strategic use of questions, aimed only at
relationships that are likely high-yield.

## Barriers-to-aiming: objection handling

- **"This takes too much time."** Encoding for the first time while learning the skill does take
  considerably longer, and this short-term cost can feel unacceptable. Measured holistically,
  however, the time lost in initial encoding is more than recovered even at early stages of skill
  competence (see the external learning-efficiency measurement article,
  /learning-support/learning-efficiency).
- **"It's too hard / too tiring."** Effective learning always takes effort; avoiding difficulty
  and discomfort is only viable for easy content at moderately low volumes. With practice, higher
  cognitive load comes to be associated with effective learning rather than aversiveness — this
  is the desirable-difficulty reframe: as skill improves, the cognitive load itself does not
  reduce, but the learner becomes better at managing it and is no longer bothered by it. Many
  learners instead grow averse to passive learning once they recognise how ineffective it is.
- **Scope guidance ("the questions I think of aren't how I'm assessed").** During encoding,
  priority goes to what the brain finds relevant — this is the shortest path to expertise.
  Retrieval can be practised in an assessment-matched way only *after* expertise is built; forcing
  encoding to match assessment format early is less efficient, because it limits encoding ability
  and therefore increases forgetting (analogy: build the road network first, then choose which
  roads to drive). Slightly exceeding a curriculum's scope to improve depth and retention is more
  efficient than staying narrowly in-scope at the cost of reduced retention or depth. Judgment,
  combined with the topic's learning objectives where available, bounds how far this
  scope-widening should go, preventing unbounded scope-creep.

## Distraction and maintaining-focus basics (Ascent I)

An extension of prior-stage focus techniques, offered as four practice techniques recommended
regardless of experience level:

1. **Intention Setting** — set specific, achievable goals for the end of the study block, written
   down physically.
2. **Ritualisation** — create a set routine that transitions the brain into high-focus mode
   automatically.
3. **Zone** — set up a study area with zero distractions.
4. **Cheat Sheet** — write down anything that causes distraction during study, then remove that
   distraction from the study zone before the next session.

Supporting references: the marshmallow study (willpower is less important than environment,
https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1972-20631-001); fMRI studies documenting distraction pathways and
their reward responses (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3304477/); a study showing
the ability to resist temptation/distraction is independent of the ability to create and maintain
focus (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/301508636); and research on pre-performance
ritualisation aiding self-regulation and automaticity in high-skill task execution
(https://journals.humankinetics.com/view/journals/jsep/24/4/article-p359.xml).

## Question-quantity shift at the Skin stage

Aim continues into the later Skin stage (the third Bear Hunter System step, which consolidates,
integrates, refines, and finalises the chunk structure built across multiple Aim-Shoot cycles).
At Skin, the guidance for Aim shifts: **start reducing total question quantity in favour of
higher quality** (source: ics-course/03-growth/05-ascent-iii/01-bear-hunter-system-skin.md). This
marks a change from Aim's earlier-stage emphasis on generating many candidate relationships
toward a later-stage emphasis on fewer, more discriminating questions once the underlying chunk
structure is largely established.

## Stage-transition context

On entering Ascent I, Aim and "maintaining focus" are the two new techniques; TLS green light,
scheduling basics, BEDS-M, SIR, and non-linear note-taking continue unchanged (non-linear
note-taking is flagged for later replacement in Ascent II). The replacement table at this
transition: TLS red light -> Bear Hunter System Aim; Prestudy basics -> Bear Hunter System Aim
(source: ics-course/03-growth/03-ascent-i/01-bear-hunter-system-aim.md).
