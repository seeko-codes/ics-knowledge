## Overview

Breaching questions is a method for efficiently identifying both known and unknown knowledge gaps late in
mastery, even when such gaps are rare. The name reflects their purpose: they are designed to "breach" through
learning plateaus. The method is used because gap-finding becomes structurally difficult at this stage for
two reasons: there are generally very few gaps left to find, and remaining gaps split into two qualitatively
different types —

- **Known gaps** — detected directly, through difficulty in recall or knowledge application.
- **Unknown gaps** — things never known in the first place; the learner may feel confident answering based on
  what they do know, while having missed an entire relevant perspective they never considered.

## Prerequisite

Breaching questions is explicitly named an advanced method: it "requires a high level of cognitive load
tolerance and the ability to do a significant level of non-linear mapping mentally." It is not recommended
before the learner has that tolerance and mental-mapping capacity in place.

## Procedure (LO-keyword cross-mapping)

1. Find the learning objectives (LOs) of the subject and list them out.
2. For each LO, list all the keywords that relate to it.
3. As each keyword is listed, mentally map how it relates to the others.
4. Pay attention to any difficulty in the mental mapping — this may indicate a **known** knowledge gap.
5. After completing this for every LO in the topic, review the keyword lists for anything missed. A keyword
   known to belong in the topic but that has not been listed and mentally mapped could indicate a known or
   unknown gap.
6. Look for keywords that appear in the list/map for one LO but not another, and explore whether they could
   also apply to those other LOs. This forces the exploration of perspectives where the keyword is relevant
   under a different LO, which is what surfaces **unknown** gaps.

## Mechanism note

The cross-mapping step (6) is the operative mechanism for unknown-gap detection: because unknown gaps are by
definition invisible to direct self-testing (the learner does not know to test for a perspective they never
considered), the method manufactures the missing perspective indirectly, by forcing every keyword to be
checked against every LO rather than only the LO it was originally listed under.
