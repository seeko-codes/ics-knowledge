## Overview

Multipass is the strategy layer for implementing the Bear Hunter System across multiple learning sessions
("passes") on large or dense topics, using the Swiss-cheese model: each pass is one layer of holes, and
multiple passes together cover almost all the material within a few days. BHS supplies the core skills and
steps for encoding any new information within a session; Multipass supplies additional guidance on *when* to
learn and a high-level direction for order control — it dictates what is learned in each session and how a
topic is divided into manageable, efficient learning blocks. Each individual pass still executes the full BHS
steps.

## Prerequisites

Multipass is explicitly an advanced system, requiring the learner to already have:

1. **Excellent order control**
2. **Inquiry-based learning** ability
3. **Effective note-writing using VPReFRE**

## Two variants

| | Inquiry-led | Objectives-led |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Order control dictated by what feels intuitively relevant, driven by the inquiry process and Aim-Shoot cycling | Order control dictated by stated learning objectives |
| **Benefits** | Feels natural and engaging; an extension of conventional BHS divided into multiple sessions, each covering a variable number of layers of learning | Learning is always directly relevant to most assessable material; time spent has a high likelihood of translating to performance results |
| **Disadvantages** | Can be less strategic under significant time pressure | Sacrifices cognitively optimal encoding for performance strategy, which can reduce retention and the ability to respond to higher-order or unanticipated "curveball" questions |
| **Use cases** | Rapid learning of high volumes under flexible time restrictions; material that needs to be fundamentally strong for future learning and application | Imminent time pressure requiring high volumes of learning with minimal study time; topics with limited long-term relevance |

Both variants prioritise the **logic and concepts layers first**, in contrast to conventional cramming, which
starts with detail layers of fluctuating relevance. As a result, Multipass requires more time to become
"performance-ready" than naive detail-first cramming — a stated tradeoff, not a hidden cost.

## Strategic benefit

Because most exam questions are derived from logic and concepts rather than isolated details, developing
those layers first (regardless of which variant is used) makes it much easier to logically extrapolate and
answer a wide range of questions, including ones not directly rehearsed.

## Supplementation rules

If time allows, adding an interleaving strategy — preferably a high-volume retrieval practice (HVRP) method
such as WPW — is recommended on top of Multipass. Learners may also need to simultaneously supplement with a
lower-order memorisation strategy such as flashcards or the modified method of loci (Mmol) to cover material
Multipass's logic/concepts-first approach does not directly drill.

## Relation to cramming principles

Multipass is introduced as *the* system used to satisfy the three principles of efficient cramming (strategic
content-skipping, sufficient retention/mastery depth, and not sacrificing sleep) — see exam-execution for the
principles themselves. The companion "cram early" principle (complete the curriculum well ahead of the
deadline, using only periodic SIR to catch gaps) is a scheduling strategy that reduces reliance on Multipass
under acute time pressure, rather than a substitute for it.
