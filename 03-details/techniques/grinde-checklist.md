# GRINDE Checklist

GRINDE maps are framed as the final and best version of a mind map — the criteria are
explicitly a completion/quality standard, not just a construction step. GRINDE is an acronym used
to refine a mind map during the Skin step; GRINDE is a mindmap-specific extension of the same
guidelines as VPReFRE, and similarities between the two are expected.

## G — Grouped

Chunk the information on the mind map, per the 2-4 Rule (see
03-details/techniques/mindmap-construction.md and the importance-based-chunking concept).

## R — Reflective

Reflect on order control, just as with VPReFRE's Reflective letter: pay attention to the order of
relationships, logic, concepts, and details, to determine whether the map reflects the way the
learner's own brain understands the topic. The mind map should fulfil the layers of learning; if
it does not, this is the opportunity to sort information into clear layers (backbone/logic →
concepts → important details). "This additional effort to make the layers clear is a
characteristic indication of an advanced level learner."

## I — Interconnected

Arrows should be interconnected, not all pointing in a single direction or purely outward. There
should be connections between chunks and concepts within the same layer, as well as between
layers, where relevant and important. Good chunking creates simple branching; good
interconnectedness extends this into a web of concepts that clearly influence each other, giving
a clear sense of purpose to the information.

Named failure mode — **wheel and spokes**: good chunking may be present, but all relationships
radiate outward from a single central hub, indicating a lack of lateral connectivity between
chunks and concepts.

## N — Non-verbal

Use doodles, visuals, and as few words as possible. An image helps retain much more information
than lines of words. Explore shorthand and contractions to form a personal abbreviation system.

## D — Directional

Maintain a flow of information and ideas across the map, so the map shows the influence of
concepts on other concepts (making it more logical). Always relate information to the big
picture, for a sense of direction. Whether a map is directional depends heavily on adherence to
the Aim step, a problem-solving mentality, and how importance-based the first (logic) layer is.

## E — Emphasised

Make the layers easy to see, using different sizes, colours, or fonts. Though it may seem
insignificant, this speeds revision and facilitates the brain's ability to create relationships;
cognitively, it requires being highly explicit about which concepts and relationships are most
important — "think of it like building mental highways and motorways."
