## Overview

BEDS-M is a five-component framework for improving focus and reducing procrastination: **B**urnt ships, **E**nvironment, **D**istraction cheat sheet, **S**cheduling, **M**inimum viable goals. The components are explicitly not of equal impact — each is ranked below by relative usefulness.

## Component impact ranking

Environment is flagged as **potentially the most powerful of all five** components — in many cases sufficient on its own to produce permanent behaviour change, and cited as one of the biggest drivers of sustainable behaviour change per behavioural-psychology research referenced in the source. The other components are adjuncts that increase Environment's effectiveness or cover cases where it alone is insufficient.

## Components

### Burnt ships strategy

Setting a high, genuinely feared consequence of failure to remove the option of retreat (named after historical generals/conquistadors who burned their own transport ships after landing, so no retreat was possible).

- **Application**: setting high stakes for a mundane failure to act — e.g., an accountability pair with a public, embarrassing consequence (the source example: standing up and shouting in a crowded hall) for non-completion. The source explicitly does not recommend this specific example literally; it illustrates the required severity.
- **Requirements**: the consequence must be high enough to be genuinely feared, and it must actually be enforced — an accountability buddy is recommended.
- **Where it fits / limits**: a useful adjunct when other methods alone are insufficient, and well-suited to short-term, urgent behaviour change. It operates at the "consequence" level of behavioural psychology, which is generally either unsustainable or inadequate alone over the long term — hence its ranking below Environment.

### Environment

If a cookie jar sits in front of someone who loves cookies, resisting it costs real motivation and willpower; even someone who feels neutral about cookies may be tempted. Controlling the environment — removing the cookie jar — removes the need to spend willpower at all.

- **Application**: make the environment conducive to success by removing anything that may cause procrastination. Removing distractions is easier and more productive than resisting them with willpower.
- **Pairing**: to make productive, targeted environment changes (rather than guessing), pair this component with the Distraction cheat sheet.
- Figure: `../research/_assets/inline-images/Cookie jar dalle.png` (illustrates why a visible temptation cue, not lack of willpower, is the actual problem).

### Distraction cheat sheet

A simple sheet of paper for tracking distractors during a study session, used to drive subsequent environment changes.

- **Procedure**: keep the sheet nearby while studying. Each time attention breaks toward a distraction, write it down rather than acting on it. When next setting up the environment, refer to the sheet to eliminate the logged distractions.
- **Usefulness**: primarily effective when paired with Environment; also increases general awareness of what affects focus, improving overall focus self-regulation as a side benefit.

### Scheduling

A basic, sustainable schedule (see the scheduling parameters in `time-priority-management.md` for the mechanics).

- **Usefulness**: when used correctly and sustainably, schedules improve focus at a psychological level via improved clarity over tasks/events, reduced cognitive burden of decision-making, reduced flow disruption, and reduced context-switching (which carries a significant cognitive-switching penalty). A basic schedule, even set loosely, is a core tool for any learner.

### Minimum viable goals (MVG)

The smallest possible action step needed to begin a larger desired behaviour, with motivation cost as close to zero as possible.

- **Procedure**: (1) start with the larger desired behaviour; (2) identify the steps needed to begin engaging in it; (3) break the steps down until the point where being unable to achieve the smallest step would feel ridiculous.
- **Worked example — couch to study desk** (7-step MVG chain): (1) take a single deep breath and look out the window; (2) take 3 more breaths; (3) stand up; (4) walk to the study room; (5) sit at the desk; (6) take out study material; (7) study for 2 minutes.
- **Usefulness and limits**: MVGs help train out existing bad habits and train in new ones. For firmly ingrained habits, only 2-3 MVGs in the chain may be sustained before reverting — this is expected, not a failure, and consistent repeated exposure produces marginal improvement over time. MVGs alone are usually insufficient when the environment is not prepared; better environment setup makes MVGs easier by removing barriers.

## Cookie-jar / distraction-sheet pairing (practice procedure)

1. Write down a list of things that are distracting — physical, digital, or people.
2. Write down a list of changes that could remove the distractions or deter/inform people in order to help maintain focus.
3. Make the changes to the environment.
4. During the next focused session, log any new distractions on the distraction sheet.
5. Adjust the environment again based on the sheet.

Repeat for at least **three sessions**; by that point, high-yield environment changes should be in place. An advanced variant: attempt to use MVGs when the urge to procrastinate arises, and log any barriers encountered on the distraction sheet (e.g., not having the right tabs already open), then fold those barriers into the next environment adjustment.

## Thinner duplicate note (30-Day-Plan "Environmental optimisation")

A later, 30-Day-Plan lesson restates the Environment component in a substantially thinner form: environmental optimisation is described as one of the most important levers for creating focus, with the same two-part heuristic — remove bad triggers and make distractions harder to access; add good triggers and make good behaviours more convenient. It carries no cookie-jar example, no distraction-sheet pairing, and no explicit "recap" framing despite covering the same ground as the fuller treatment above. It is folded in here as confirmation of the same principle at a later curriculum stage, not as additional content.
