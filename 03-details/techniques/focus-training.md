## Mechanism

Focus training is a mindfulness-meditation-based technique. Emotional self-regulation and sustained focus are treated as a matter of *preventing* the brain from being hijacked by thoughts and emotions, not of *forcing* it to concentrate. The skill trained is letting go of an intrusive thought and returning attention to a chosen anchor — repeated many times per session — not the elimination of intrusive thoughts.

Conceptually, training has two components: (1) placing oneself in a situation where attention naturally drifts, usually one free of external distraction (the same reason the mind tends to wander before sleep); (2) practising letting go of whatever arises and bringing attention back.

## Procedure

1. Sit somewhere comfortable, away from distractions. Close the eyes.
2. Take slow, deep breaths in and out.
3. Focus on the sensation of each breath — airflow, the body's movement, and any other sensation associated with breathing.
4. After a few seconds, other thoughts will arise and attention will wander. Each time this happens, acknowledge the thought and let go of it without pursuing or fixating on it, then bring attention gently back to the breath.
5. Repeat this process for **15 to 20 minutes**.

**Guidance on the letting-go step**: do not deliberately try to *stop* having distracting thoughts. Observe them and let them pass — like clouds floating in the sky or leaves floating down a stream — without holding onto them, reaching for them, or giving them sustained attention.

## Dosing and progression

- **Therapeutic dose**: 20 minutes daily for 30 days is the dose generally associated with significant improvement.
- **Minimum-effective dose**: 10 minutes daily has produced observable effects in program experience. Consistency is emphasised over duration.
- **Baseline mind-wandering rate**: it is normal for the mind to wander every 3-4 seconds, even in trained practitioners.
- **Long-run ceiling**: even after years of practice, no more than roughly 10 seconds of "mental silence" is typical — but this represents a tripling of baseline focus ability, not a failure of the method.
- Anchoring the practice into a fixed daily routine as a new habit is recommended (see `habit-formation.md` for the cue-response mechanism this relies on).

## Competence target

At the Base Camp checkpoint, the target competency is: consistent daily practice with easy tolerance of feelings of "boredom" or "restlessness"; a felt increase in general (not just immediately post-practice) focus; and intermittent, informal use of the letting-go skill throughout the day, not only during dedicated sessions.

## Warning

Because focus training is a form of mindfulness meditation, it can resurface traumatic memories in learners with a history of past trauma. Learners aware of such a history are advised to engage in this practice under the guidance of a therapist or psychologist; most trained therapists are experienced in guiding clients through this specific technique.

## Application note

Focus training is also used as one of the two active-relaxation break options within OFF-rest timing (see `off-rest-timing.md`), and as the primary attention-recentering skill applied more broadly under neuroticism management (see `mindset-emotion.md`).
