## The 5-step tracking procedure

**Step 1 — Anchor the goal.** Identify a meaningful, challenging goal to achieve in the medium
term: **9 months to 3 years** out.

**Step 2 — Dissect the goal.** What knowledge, attributes, skills, processes, or resources are
needed to achieve it? Consider: mental health; focus and concentration; time management and
prioritisation; studying skills. Ask "how sure am I?" of each — this is an explicit check on
whether the dissection is based on reliable information or on unreliable sources/assumptions.

**Step 3 — Evaluate target level.** For each factor from Step 2, rate 0–10 the level required to
reach the goal, and describe explicitly what that rating means in concrete terms.

**Step 4 — Evaluate current level.** For each factor, rate 0–10 the current level and justify the
rating as objectively as possible, guarding against over- or under-estimation.

**Step 5 — Identify the 1%.** Define the next 1% step toward the target for each factor.

**Review cadence.** On future reviews, typically only Steps 4 and 5 need to be repeated; Steps
1–3 (the goal, its dissection, and the target levels) tend to remain stable.

**Parallel-work limit.** Do not attempt to improve more than **2** skills or attributes
simultaneously — this causes overload and hinders improvement (see the numeric variance against
the 2–3 figure used elsewhere, ../../02-concepts/marginal-gains.md).

**Progression stages.** Marginal gains typically progress from **knowledge** → **actions** →
**behaviours/habits/position**. Position is the degree of control held over achieving the goal;
a better position reduces risk. Early stages require acquiring knowledge of what action to take;
next, action is taken to begin the improvement process; over time, actions change behaviours,
habits, or position.

**Decision rule when uncertain (applies at any step):** if enough is known to attempt something
and risk a mistake, the next step is to experiment and reflect on the attempt; if not enough is
known even to attempt it, the next step is to gain more information first. Gaining information is
itself the most valuable marginal gain when genuinely uncertain — action cannot be taken before
knowing what action to take, so identifying uncertainties is often the best *first* marginal gain
for a difficult goal. A comprehensive list of every needed knowledge/attribute/skill/process is not
required at the outset — only what seems clearly necessary as the next step; early-stage progress
is expected to be confusingly broad, and this uncertainty is itself normal rather than a sign of
doing it wrong.

## Worked numeric example

Goal: "I want to achieve at least 90% in my end-of-year exam."

**Dissection** (needs, recursively broken down): (1) adequate mental health — knowledge of how to
maintain it, self-awareness of what causes it to fluctuate, techniques, and practice using those
techniques; (2) good focus and concentration — fewer distractions (itself needing knowledge of
what the distractions are and skills to reduce them) and ability to enter deep flow (needing
knowledge of what triggers flow, techniques, and practice); (3) excellent time management and
prioritisation — better time-management methods (flexible, sustainable, consistent) and better
task prioritisation (knowledge, self-awareness of prioritisation mistakes, tools/processes,
practice); (4) excellent learning skills — high-quality encoding (knowledge, techniques, practice)
and high-quality retrieval practice (knowledge, techniques, practice). Cross-cutting needs for all
of the above: sufficient feedback on technique accuracy, frequent reflection/self-feedback, and a
growth mindset to experiment without being paralysed by "what if" uncertainty.

**Target ratings** (each with stated rationale): mental health 7/10 (good enough to stay
optimal/performing, not invulnerable to all stress); focus/concentration 8/10 (must produce
meaningful work in set blocks given limited time, but does not need to hold in any setting since
study happens mostly in controlled environments); time and task management 7/10 (schedule is not
very busy, so some room for error is acceptable); learning skills 9/10 (exam is very difficult and
competitive, requiring deep mastery of all core system parts); feedback 8/10; self-reflection 8/10
(never actively worked on this before, struggles with it including identifying marginal gains, and
needs to self-correct quickly since external feedback is slower); growth mindset 8/10 (some fixed-
mindset tendencies present; must be able to experiment while unsure of correctness, which currently
feels uncomfortable).

**Current ratings** (each with justification): mental health 4/10 (handles some stress, but
overwhelmed easily; some knowledge/process, not much); focus/concentration 3/10 (distracted and
procrastinates easily; deep focus happens but is inconsistent; little knowledge/experience
managing it); time and task management 6/10 (mostly does what's needed, but struggles to complete
everything and adapt when plans change; has some knowledge but no practised technique); learning
skills 5/10 (highly variable academic performance, averaging 70% on tests, occasional 90%+ but
never confident; large study-time investment does not translate predictably into results; some
knowledge and some learned techniques, but little practice and uncertainty about where to start);
feedback 2/10 (rarely asks for or feels comfortable with feedback, despite knowing where/how to
get it); self-reflection 2/10 (struggles with the process, confused, does not know how to start);
growth mindset 3/10 (uncertain about trying new things, tends to ask many questions to gain
certainty first, delaying experimentation for days or weeks; knows what growth mindset means but
not how to develop it, with limited experience trying).

**Finding the 1%** (each with rationale tied directly to the Step-4 current-level justification):
- Mental health: learn about what affects mental health and processes to improve it.
- Focus/concentration: learn about what affects focus and processes to improve it.
- Time/task management: practise and apply known techniques and observe results.
- Learning skills: apply one or two known learning techniques and observe results.
- Feedback: ask for feedback on attempted work at least once.
- Self-reflection: 20 minutes of self-reflection every Wednesday and Saturday, reviewing Steps 4
  and 5; the rationale notes that "I do not know how to start" is often itself a sign that the
  right next step is simply to make a best attempt and improve after, rather than continuing to
  think about how to start (which can become an endless, unproductive process).
- Growth mindset: try a technique that is not fully understood and see what happens, deliberately
  surfacing mistakes and barriers not yet known (framed explicitly against the conscious-
  competence model — this is a way to move along the unconscious-incompetence stage rather than
  delay it via more certainty-seeking).

**Final prioritisation.** Because pursuing more than three gains at once risks overwhelm, three
are selected as the highest-priority/most-limiting: focus and concentration, growth mindset, and
learning skills. (This selection of *three* sits in explicit tension with the article's own
stated "not more than 2" rule earlier in the same procedure — flagged, not resolved, in the
source.)

## Combination with the 30-Day Plan

Comparison table and full concept treatment: ../../02-concepts/marginal-gains.md. Combination
procedure (3 steps):
1. **Start with the 30-Day Plan.** (a) Identify a medium-term goal and break it into the most
   important short-term, performance-based goals. (b) Explore other factors affecting behaviour
   and performance — limiting habits, environment.
2. **Supplement with Marginal Gains Tracking & Stacking.** (a) Prioritise the 2–3 most important
   skills/processes/attributes for the medium-term and performance goals. (b) Track and stack 1%
   improvements toward steady attainment of these goals.
3. **Make pace adjustments** and continue marginal gains in the required areas.

**Worked example — Zaheer** (A-level exam, 4 months out): 30-Day Plan step identifies the
important processes for the performance goals as scheduling, high-efficiency encoding, and
interleaved retrieval practice, and separately identifies procrastination as a limiting habit
requiring a scripted action. Marginal Gains Tracking & Stacking step finds the weakest link to be
ineffective interleaved retrieval practice plus procrastination; the chosen 1% improvement is
practising relational teaching every other day as interleaved retrieval, combined with
consciously practising the BEDS-M framework for procrastination for one week. Daily reflection is
used to make tiny adjustments to the technique as new mistakes and opportunities for improvement
are identified.
