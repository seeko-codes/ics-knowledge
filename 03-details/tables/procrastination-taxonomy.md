## Root mechanism

Every form of procrastination reduces to the same underlying condition: **toll > tolerance** — a task's emotional toll exceeds the current tolerance for it. The six mechanisms below differ in *why* the toll is high or the tolerance is low, and therefore require different, matched remedies rather than one generic fix.

## Six mechanisms × remedies

| # | Mechanism | Characterisation | Matched remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aversive task process | The process of doing the task itself is aversive; hyping oneself up or exposure therapy alone is not sufficient to overcome this. | Flatten the emotional spike by "ramping in" via the Zeigarnik effect — start with a small, incomplete action that creates an open loop pulling attention back toward finishing. Pair with giving genuine, explicit permission to stop early at a small checkpoint (the source's illustrative phrase: "permission to stop at two socks"), rather than requiring full completion before any relief is allowed. |
| 2 | Dreading the implication of the task (perfectionism-as-insecurity) | Reframed as insecurity, not high standards: the task is avoided because of what it might reveal about competence, not because the standard itself is demanding. | Level out the risk with frequent feedback and deliberate, imperfect drafts, so no single output carries the full weight of self-evaluation. |
| 3 | Decisional procrastination | The task is stalled at the decision stage rather than the execution stage. | Time-box the decision itself — a hard deadline for *deciding*, not only for doing — rather than allowing the decision to be worked through indefinitely. |
| 4 | Pressure-dependent procrastination | Action only occurs once external pressure (an imminent deadline) is high enough; partly driven by the brain undervaluing distant-horizon rewards relative to near-term ones. What feels like "strategic delay" should be assumed to be rationalisation unless proven otherwise. | Wean off dependence on pressure via earlier self-imposed deadlines and reverse goal-setting (see `../techniques/reverse-goal-setting.md`) — working backward from the true deadline to set intermediate deadlines further out. |
| 5 | Generalised anxiety | Procrastination as a downstream symptom of anxiety broader than the specific task. | Professional support. |
| 6 | ADHD/ASD | Attentional-profile-driven procrastination. | Professional support; see also `../techniques/adhd-adjustments.md` for course-technique-level adjustments once clinical support is in place. |

## Adjunct tactic

Because the prefrontal cortex fatigues across the day, important decisions (most relevant to mechanism 3) are best made before that fatigue has set in.
