## The 7 levels

Presented in the source lesson as "a recap of the levels" (summarised from the lesson's video
component):

1. Memorise
2. Try to understand
3. Rate the importance of each keyword
4. Group keywords together based on similarities
5. Find different kinds of groups, based on different similarities
6. Rate the importance and relevance of the different kinds of groups, deciding on a group judged
   best
7. Connect groups together to show how they influence each other

Most techniques taught in the program can be performed at different levels of this ladder; some
techniques make deeper processing easier to reach, or force it outright. The prescribed response,
once comfortable at a level, is to recalibrate and move to the next, deeper level.

## Calibration / level-identification protocol (practice instructions)

1. Take a set of keywords to learn. Recommended size: at least 10, up to 20.
2. Engage with each level of processing in turn and try to identify which level feels
   comfortable.
3. At each level, note: how difficult it is, how active the brain feels, and how confident the
   learner feels about depth of understanding and retention.
4. Once the current level is known, the next level is also known (it is the next rung up the
   ladder).
5. Going forward, operate at the identified next level. Watch for — and self-correct — drops back
   into lower levels, which especially tend to occur when tired or rushed.

## Relation to retrieval practice

The source lesson cross-references retrieval practice as one of the core parts of learning
(actively recalling information to strengthen long-term memory), distinct from but complementary
to the processing-level calibration described above.
