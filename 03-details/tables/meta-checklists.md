A recurring table pattern appears attached to multiple techniques across the corpus: a two-column
"How it can feel" / "Cognitive outcomes" table, explicitly framed (in near-identical language each
time) as a metacognition tool — connecting the learner's subjective experience of a technique to
what that experience means at a cognitive-processing level, so that discomfort can be interpreted
correctly (as productive load) instead of read as a sign of failure. This entry collects every
instance of the pattern found within the source material reviewed for this writer's scope, plus
the one instance named but never captured.

## Higher- vs. lower-order learning (general form)

Source: `ics-learning-support/encoding/higher-order-learning.md`. Framed as "What does
higher-order learning feel like?" rather than as a named technique's checklist, but the same
feel-to-outcome structure.

| Higher-order learning | Lower-order learning |
|---|---|
| Involves comparing, contrasting, evaluating, and forming networks and relationships. | Involves rote-memorising, repeating, and trying to understand information in isolation (without comparing and creating networks). |
| Feels challenging, mentally effortful, and potentially confusing. Learners often go back and forth between their material and spend lots of time thinking deeply and asking questions. | Feels tedious, time-consuming, mentally straightforward, and potentially boring or sleep-inducing. |

## Aim (Bear Hunter System, Ascent I)

Source: `ics-course/03-growth/03-ascent-i/02-how-to-aim.md`. Framed identically: "The table below
outlines how using this technique can feel and what these feelings mean at a cognitive level."

| How it can feel | Cognitive outcomes |
|---|---|
| Same as for the Traffic-Light System (cross-referenced to the TLS meta-checklist). | Same as for the Traffic-Light System (cross-referenced to the TLS meta-checklist). |
| Overwhelming, due to the number of relationships discovered while answering "Why is it important?" | Rapidly creates a network of possible relationships to refine. |
| Confusing, as there are many possible non-linear structures for the main concepts, and it takes significant effort to decide which structure makes the most sense. | Adds a high degree of prioritisation and evaluation (one of the highest orders of learning). |
| Time-consuming, because each keyword or concept requires deep examination. | Deep examination consolidates learning. This feeling fades as speed and efficiency gradually increase; the brain becomes better at creating networks, prioritising them, and deciding on the best hypothetical structure. |

## Intuitive chunking (Ascent III)

Source: `ics-course/03-growth/05-ascent-iii/05-intuitive-chunking.md`.

| How it can feel | Cognitive outcomes |
|---|---|
| Feeling overwhelmed, similar to when first learning the Aim step. This happens because of having to think about the best way to represent and label chunks. | Forces prioritisation and evaluation at a higher level than previous versions of the technique. Creates deeper, more memorable structures. |
| Suddenly feeling slower and less fluent than before. | Caused by the above: the standard for high-quality chunking and encoding has increased. This becomes easier with practice, as with the other entries in this table. |

## Whole-Part-Whole / WPW (Camp II)

Source: `ics-course/03-growth/08-camp-ii/03-wpw.md`.

| How it can feel | Cognitive outcomes |
|---|---|
| Very slow to teach; hyper-awareness of how one is speaking or which points are chosen to explain next. | Forces a deep, multi-order evaluation of the knowledge structure; the mental schema is tested against higher-order criteria such as "What makes the most sense?" and "Why is this important?" |
| Anxiety from frequently finding gaps in knowledge. | A good sign: it allows identification of gaps that may have been hidden by the illusion of explanatory depth. |

## Pattern summary

Every instance found follows the same two-part logic: (1) a feeling that would, read naively,
suggest the technique is failing or that the learner is regressing (overwhelm, confusion, slowed
fluency, anxiety, time cost) is (2) reinterpreted as evidence that the technique is correctly
forcing a higher order of cognitive engagement than the learner's prior habitual level, and that
the discomfort will recede with practice as the new processing level becomes habitual. This is
the general mechanism recorded in the self-correction-loop basis as reading subjective signals
("effort, confusion, fluency, feelings") as diagnostic data rather than error signals, applied
here specifically to cognitive-load self-regulation during higher-order technique acquisition.
