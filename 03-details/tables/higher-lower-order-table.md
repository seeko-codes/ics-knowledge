| | Higher-order learning | Lower-order learning |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | Creates relationships between facts and concepts to form a *network* of knowledge. | Focuses on remembering and understanding *without* actively comparing it with anything else. |
| **How** | All higher-order learning techniques involve forming relationships by: (1) *Comparing* information with each other to find similarities and differences; (2) *Judging the importance* of those similarities and differences. | Lower-order learning is often simply the absence of higher-order learning. It is usually driven by old habits of learning that emphasise repetition, memorisation, and isolated understanding. |
| **Examples** | Self-questioning list (see below). | Self-questioning list (see below). |

## Example self-questioning lists

**Higher-order habits of thinking** — the learner should be asking:
- How is this related to this other concept?
- How is this similar or different to what I already know?
- What is an analogy for this?
- How does this fit within the big picture?
- What function does it serve?
- What are the trends?

**Lower-order habits of thinking** involve asking:
- What is the meaning of this?
- What are they trying to say?
- I feel like this is important. I need to repeat this to remember it.
