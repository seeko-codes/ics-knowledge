## Statement

Procrastination is not a single behaviour with a single cause. It occurs whenever a task's emotional toll exceeds current tolerance ("toll > tolerance"), but the source of that toll differs by mechanism, and each mechanism requires a different, matched remedy rather than a single generic fix (e.g., "just get started," "use more willpower"). Naming procrastination generically prevents effective troubleshooting, because it hides which specific trigger-response pair is actually operating (L10), and several of the six mechanisms are fundamentally emotional-appraisal problems rather than conditioning problems (L11).

## Six-mechanism taxonomy (canonical)

The most sophisticated treatment in the corpus decomposes procrastination into six distinct mechanisms, each with a matched remedy:

1. **Aversive task process** — the process of doing the task itself is aversive. Hyping oneself up or exposure therapy alone is insufficient. Remedy: flatten the emotional spike by "ramping in" via the Zeigarnik effect (starting with a trivially small, incomplete action that creates an open loop pulling attention back to finish it), combined with giving explicit, genuine permission to stop early at a small checkpoint (rather than requiring full completion before any relief is allowed).
2. **Dreading the implication of the task (perfectionism-as-insecurity)** — perfectionism is reframed as insecurity, not high standards: the task is avoided because what it might reveal about competence is threatening, not because the standard itself is demanding. Remedy: level out the risk with frequent feedback and deliberate, imperfect drafts, so no single output carries the full weight of self-evaluation.
3. **Decisional procrastination** — the task is stalled at the decision stage rather than the execution stage. Remedy: time-box the decision itself (a hard deadline for deciding, not just for doing), rather than letting it be worked through indefinitely.
4. **Pressure-dependent procrastination** — action only occurs once external pressure (an imminent deadline) is high enough; this is driven partly by the brain undervaluing distant-horizon rewards relative to near-term ones. A key diagnostic caution: what feels like "strategic delay" should be assumed to be rationalisation unless proven otherwise. Remedy: wean off dependence on pressure via earlier self-imposed deadlines and reverse goal-setting (working backward from the outcome to set intermediate deadlines further from the true one).
5. **Generalised anxiety** — procrastination as a downstream symptom of anxiety broader than the task itself. Remedy: professional support.
6. **ADHD/ASD** — attentional-profile-driven procrastination. Remedy: professional support (see also the ADHD/ASD adjustment protocol, `03-details/techniques/adhd-adjustments.md`, for course-technique-level adjustments once any clinical support is in place).

A practical adjunct noted alongside the taxonomy: because the prefrontal cortex fatigues across the day, important decisions (relevant especially to mechanism 3) are best made before that fatigue sets in.

Full mechanism × remedy mapping in table form: `03-details/tables/procrastination-taxonomy.md`.

## Scaffold: earlier tip-list and intermediate lessons

Before the six-mechanism taxonomy, the program teaches a simpler, quick-start layer whose content is preserved here as scaffold rather than discarded, since it captures two mechanism ideas not otherwise made explicit above.

**Quick tips (starting-friction focus)**:
- Make a list and break tasks down — reduces overwhelm and sharpens focus by making tasks specific.
- Set up the environment for success — behaviour is shaped by environment (see `habit-formation.md`).
- Get started beforehand — leans into perfectionist tendencies to get momentum rolling before resistance builds.
- Group up — leverages social accountability pressure.
- Aim for a personal best, not perfection — meets the learner where they are rather than requiring an idealised standard.

These tips are explicitly self-flagged in the source as a "quick introduction," superseded by fuller technique coverage later.

**Task-aversion theory (willpower framing)**: procrastination is driven by many independent factors influencing motivation (mood, sleep/energy, interest, sense of purpose, punishments/rewards, autonomy, identity/values alignment, competence, psychological reserve, physical wellbeing) that cannot realistically all be controlled day to day. Since motivation fluctuates for reasons outside deliberate control, plans must be built to succeed even on a low-motivation day — which is the basis for preferring willpower-enhanced systems (habit and environment design) over willpower-dependent ones (see `habit-formation.md` for the full willpower-enhanced/dependent comparison, which the six-mechanism taxonomy builds on).

**BEDS-M** (Burnt ships, Environment, Distraction cheat sheet, Scheduling, Minimum viable goals) is the fuller scaffold framework taught between the quick tips and the six-mechanism taxonomy, aimed primarily at aversive-task-process and starting-friction procrastination. Full spec: `03-details/techniques/beds-m.md`. It is superseded for habit-formation purposes by PEER-Peer (see `habit-formation.md`) but its Distraction-cheat-sheet/Environment pairing remains the canonical distraction-handling ladder (see `attention-focus.md`).

## Independence

Without L10, procrastination has no cue/environment mechanism to intervene on for mechanisms 1-4 — remedies would default to willpower exhortation. Without L11, the perfectionism-as-insecurity, generalised-anxiety, and toll>tolerance appraisal mechanisms have no explanatory basis; L10 alone would misclassify these as simple conditioning problems rather than appraisal-gated ones.
