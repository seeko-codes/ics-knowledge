## Mastery levels

Knowledge mastery is measured on a 5-level composite of Bloom's revised taxonomy and SOLO
taxonomy. Full level-by-level specification (descriptors, common methods, what each level is
commonly tested by): ../03-details/tables/mastery-levels.md.

The **Level 3 → Level 4 jump** is singled out as having a categorically different impact:
Level 3 (identifying relationships, similarities, and differences) is rated "moderately helpful";
Level 4 (making value judgments and evaluating the overall importance/relevance of those
relationships) is rated "extremely helpful — forces substantially deeper encoding and
processing." This threshold feeds directly into the learning-efficiency calculation below, since
partial mastery is computed per level.

## Learning-efficiency equation

**Learning efficiency = Total mastery and retention ÷ Total time spent learning.** Full equation,
definitions, 6-step calculation procedure, and worked numeric example:
../03-details/tables/learning-efficiency.md.

"Amount covered per session" is explicitly rejected as a meaningful efficiency metric: it ignores
retention, ignores the time cost of relearning forgotten material, and ignores the quality of
learning or the ability to use the knowledge in different ways.

## Common measurement mistakes

Two commonly-assumed inverse relationships are named and corrected: **does spending less time
studying always mean more efficiency?** (posed directly, with the implied answer being no — time
saved is not efficiency gained if mastery/retention drop proportionally or more); and **does
reduced retention always mean less efficiency?** (also posed as a nuance, not a simple yes —
reduced retention does not automatically imply the underlying learning process was inefficient).

Two named measurement methods:
- **Method 1 — "proper form"** (a bicep-curl analogy): recommended for more experienced learners,
  who can judge whether their learning technique has good "form" directly; the source notes this
  is inherently hard to assess for oneself.
- **Method 2 — self-testing and gathering data**: an explicit, testable, evidence-based
  alternative to relying on self-judged form.

Higher levels of mastery are reported to make it easier to subsequently pick up related *lower*
levels of mastery — a transfer effect in the mastery-level ladder, not just a one-way dependency
from lower to higher levels. Two methods for testing higher-order ("evaluate"-level) mastery are
named — one of them is "advanced group study" — but neither is detailed in the captured text.

## Marginal-gains tracking metrics

The marginal-gains tracking procedure (../03-details/techniques/marginal-gains-tracking.md) uses
its own **0–10 rating scale** for target-level and current-level self-assessment — distinct from
the skills-audit's 1–5 scale (../03-details/techniques/skills-audit-leep.md). This numeric-scale
mismatch across the two measurement instruments is not reconciled anywhere in the captured
record.
