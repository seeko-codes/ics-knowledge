The Bear Hunter System is the program's umbrella encoding system: three sequential steps (Aim,
Shoot, Skin) that stack together earlier inquiry-based-learning and note-taking skills into one
system, and that eventually collapse into a single fluid cycle.

## Aim

Aim is an intermediate-level inquiry-based-learning skill, building on the TLS red-light skill.
Full mechanism and question-quality criteria belong to the inquiry-based-learning /
question-quality concept cluster; only the step's role within BHS is recorded here. Aim's
purpose (three items): (1) make new information relevant and form networks immediately; (2)
activate higher-order learning as early as possible; (3) create chunks and relationships that are
robust, versatile, and suited to retrieval needs. It operates on two core questions — "Why is
this important?" and "How is this related to ___?" — which are conceptually distinct despite
surface similarity: "why important" pushes to Evaluate-level thinking and improves
memorability/intuitiveness; "how related" surfaces relationships that bias/preconceptions would
otherwise hide, and can force re-evaluation of the whole chunk structure.

Prerequisite framing: before starting Ascent I (where Aim is introduced), the corpus recommends
reaching "moderate to high conscious competency" in thirteen prior skills/techniques (higher-order
thinking, higher-order learning, non-linear notes, inquiry-based learning via TLS, backbone
forming, spaced interleaved retrieval, Kolb's cycle, marginal-gains mindset, and self-management
techniques).

## Shoot

Shoot is the second BHS step, an upgraded version of the TLS green-light step. "During Aim, we
prime our brain to find meaningful relationships by asking importance-based questions. During
Shoot, we satiate that curiosity to clarify relationships, add detail, explore further
relationships, make judgments on chunks and relationships, and focus on constructing our map."

- **Not-every-question-answered rule**: not every question has to be answered perfectly; some
  questions raised will be too complex or advanced to answer fully. The learning occurs in the
  attempt to answer, which reinforces the relationships involved.
- **"Aimed properly → shooting easy" diagnostic**: if Aim was done properly, Shooting is easy.
  Shooting becomes difficult when old bad habits or lower-order, linear learning interfere.
  Lower-order habits can cause impaired order control. Using the non-linear note-taking system
  of Shoot helps anchor thoughts and keep them focused. Shoot difficulty is therefore diagnostic
  of upstream Aim quality or persisting linear habits, not of Shoot itself.

### Barriers to Shooting (objection handling)

Documented mistakes that have blocked adoption of the Aim-Shoot method, each with its counter-mechanism:

- **"Aim-Shoot takes too much time"** — map-building creates more connections and saves net time; reverting to memorising pages of text is the actual time sink (a deathly-spiral warning). Map-building also speeds up substantially with proficiency. The learning-efficiency measurement (see measurement concept) is the recommended arbiter of whether the method truly takes longer or only seems to.
- **"Aim-Shoot is too difficult"** — the difficulty is prolonged uncertainty about one's knowledge; the certainty produced by traditional methods is an illusion of learning that yields inconsistent performance, frustration, and performance anxiety. Making and finding mistakes is integral; absence of difficulty usually signals the technique is not being applied to standard.
- **"What if something is missed?"** — more is missed by not using a map with layers; order-control jumping does not create gaps because the full BHS combined with SIR closes them.

## Skin

Skin is the final BHS step, exclusively refining higher-order learning at the Evaluate level.
Chunk structure evolves and changes to reflect updated understanding across multiple Aim-Shoot
cycles before a comprehensive view of all relationships emerges; Skin is where that structure is
consolidated, integrated, refined, and judged into its final form: "After all this time, it's
finally time to create some more certainty!" Skin's technique output is the GRINDE map (see
mind-mapping-grinde). At the Skin stage, Aim usage should also shift toward fewer, higher-quality
questions rather than high question volume.

## Aim-Shoot cycling

Once proficiency with Aim and Shoot exists as individual steps, a learner can (and often already
does, without explicit instruction) begin transitioning between the two seamlessly in a
continuous cycle. This is explicitly gated by competence, not taught as a discrete procedure:
Aim-Shoot cycling is often something that develops as a result of upper conscious competence in
each individual step. No formal cycling procedure is given beyond continued practice, after
which the ability to cycle should develop naturally.

The most explicit statement of the cycle as an actual step sequence in the corpus is
student-proposed, not ICS-authored (Live Clinic 30; Justin's confirmation is not captured — the
clinic answer exists only in DRM-locked video):

1. Organize keywords into topics of the textbook.
2. AIM questions.
3. SHOOT — answer questions and importance-base-chunk.
4. AIM (how chunks are related) → SHOOT relationship between chunks = Mindmap Backbone.
5. AIM-SHOOT between sub-chunks and other keywords = Mindmap structure.
6. AIM-SHOOT all important details/rest of keywords = Final Mindmap.

The student appends a check step ("Check Mindmap with VPReFRE") and glosses: AIM = "why is it
important" or "how [does it] relate" questions; constant swapping between the two steps; the
pipeline is understood to incorporate layers of learning and order control throughout. The same
submission reports subject-specific variants: for math, "importance based chunking based on
applications," with important details being the equations that help understand the concepts; for
English, importance-based chunks are the themes dictated by the author, with important details
being the techniques used within those themes. Because this pipeline is student-proposed and
unconfirmed in text, it should be read as the corpus's most concrete illustration of what
Aim-Shoot cycling looks like in practice, not as an ICS-authored procedure.

## Relation to Ultralearning (Scott Young)

At the Skin-step lesson, the corpus cites Scott Young's *Ultralearning* (book and YouTube video)
as related prior art, with the explicit note that "the concepts discussed in Ultralearning are
incorporated and further refined in the BHS."

## Hipshot (speed variant)

Hipshot is a Base-Camp-stage speed variant of Aim-Shoot cycling, requiring near-unconscious
competence in traditional BHS as a prerequisite. Full mechanism, the 6-step practice procedure,
and the three implementation tiers (Standard/Accelerated/Ultra-accelerated) are documented in
03-details/techniques/hipshot.md.
