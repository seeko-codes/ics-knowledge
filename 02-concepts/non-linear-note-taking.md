## Notes as a thinking surface

Note-taking method matters less than the thinking process used while producing notes. Good
note-taking helps the brain think by reducing unhelpful and extraneous cognitive load
(offloading); bad note-taking causes avoidance of thinking, which reduces learning effectiveness.
Notes serve two functions simultaneously: offloading (freeing working-memory budget by
externalising structure) and relationship exploration (the act of representing relationships is
itself the active-processing step). Non-linear note-taking places a higher priority on
relationships between pieces of information than on the information itself, which is what
produces higher retention and more active cognitive processing than linear notes. Wordier notes
are usually less processed and therefore less effective; typing exacerbates this because it is
easier to produce high volumes of low-processing text than freehand writing allows.

## VPReFRE definition and the efficiency threshold

VPReFRE is the six-component checklist for note-taking quality: **V**isual, **P**rocessed,
**Re**lational, **F**reehand, **R**eflective, **E**fficient. A mind map done correctly will
satisfy this checklist. Full per-letter criteria and rationale are in
03-details/techniques/vprefre-checklist.md; in compact form:

- **Visual** — reduce words, increase pictures/symbols/doodles/shorthand/diagrams.
- **Processed** — process information as a problem, by asking questions (ties directly to the
  Aim step of the Bear Hunter System).
- **Relational** — always relate information to other information rather than remembering
  isolated pieces; done via mind maps.
- **Freehand** — freehand writing forces more processing than typing and represents
  relationships better.
- **Reflective** — periodically check that notes are not in the same order as the source
  (textbook/lecture); reorder to match how the learner's own brain organises the topic.
- **Efficient** — the explicit threshold: forgetting more than 30% of the information written in
  one's notes indicates something wrong with how the previous points of VPReFRE are being
  applied. Below this threshold, the process is diagnosed as broken upstream (in V, P, Re, or F),
  not in the Efficient check itself.

## Sophistication ladder (recorded in scaffold)

The corpus records an explicit progression of note-taking sophistication: **linear** (worst,
lowest retention, especially when typed and near-verbatim) **< Cornell** (a beginner-level
technique — superior to the typical linear methods but less effective than the non-linear
methods taught later in the program, and does not itself transition to more efficient note-taking)
**< Collecting/Processing** (an explicitly transitionary technique used only to break the habit
of passive linear note-taking; skippable once already comfortable with non-linear notes) **<
non-linear processed note-taking** (VPReFRE, canonical) **< GRINDE** (the mind-map-specific final
extension of VPReFRE used at the Skin step — see mind-mapping-grinde).

## Delayed note-taking / discomfort-is-functional principle

Continuous note-taking — writing while still consuming the material — is a self-sabotaging habit
reinforced by most formal education. Research on cognitive load theory and freeform-vs-typed
note-taking indicates that thinking about and making sense of consumed material *before* writing
any notes improves retention, depth of understanding, and assessment performance. Delaying
note-taking is uncomfortable: "trying to figure things out" and make sense of information
naturally feels confusing and mentally uncomfortable. To avoid that discomfort, most students
write large volumes of notes for a false sense of security — but doing so removes the brain's
incentive to process the information, producing more passive and ineffective learning and a
larger total revision burden later. Conversely, tolerating the discomfort and using confusion as
fuel to actively make sense of information enables a faster, more efficient learning state. This
principle is functionally identical to VPReFRE's Processed/Relational criteria applied to
*timing*, not just content.

## Arrows-vs-lines rule

Arrows require a deeper level of processing than lines because they force explicit specification
of the *nature* of a relationship (one-way, two-way, curly-bracket, or a deliberately non-arrow
line), whereas lines only indicate a general, unprioritised awareness of connection. This single
notational choice activates higher-order learning: any lines-only diagram is compatible with
multiple distinct arrow-combination interpretations, so using only lines hides the differentiated
relationship information that arrows would reveal. Decision rule: use arrows whenever
directionality or causality exists or should actively be considered; lines remain acceptable only
to indicate that information is connected without directionality or cause/effect — typically at
lower levels of detail, where facts exist as undifferentiated components within a chunk.

## Drawing/doodling

Drawing skill is trainable like any other skill; artistic quality is not required. Sufficient
drawing ability only needs to activate the visual-processing, artistic-expression, and
abstraction functions of the brain — not produce output any third party would judge as good art.

## Hemisphere-myth correction

The video accompanying VPReFRE's Visual sublesson claims the dominant hemisphere handles rational
thinking and the non-dominant hemisphere handles abstract thinking. An explicit in-text "Lesson
correction" flags this as not well supported by current evidence: different brain areas have
different functions (established via extensive imaging); hemispheres show no relationship to
personality; there is no established *cognitive/information-processing* hemispheric dominance
(sensory and motor dominance are well established, but this is not the same claim); a general
hemisphere preference has been demonstrated, but other brain areas also activate in ways
inconsistent with a strict lateralisation theory. Learning-styles theory (matching teaching to a
visual/auditory/kinesthetic preference) is characterised across multiple citations as largely
mythical/unsupported. Multimodal learning itself *is* evidence-supported — but not proven to be
because of hemispheric specialisation specifically: "it may not practically matter... since
incorporating multimodal learning has been shown to have a benefit, regardless of why."

## App recommendations (mechanism-relevant only)

A note-taking app matters only insofar as it enables the right cognitive process: (1) it must
facilitate the correct cognitive processes for learning (most important) — concretely, an
infinite canvas plus convenient, fast freehand drawing with a digital stylus; (2) good
organisational features for managing multiple notes (less important). Most commercial note-taking
apps are designed around what sells rather than learning research, which is why many are
restrictive for this purpose. (Brand-by-brand product comparisons are not reproduced here as
non-mechanism content; see ics-course/03-growth/04-ascent-ii/02-effective-note-taking.md and
ics-learning-support/miscellaneous/recommended-apps.md for the full product lists, which are
pure-scaffolding/commercial content.)
