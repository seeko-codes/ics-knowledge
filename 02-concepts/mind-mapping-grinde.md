Mind mapping (referred to in later stages as "chunkmaps") is the map-specific application of
non-linear note-taking, and GRINDE is its final, most refined form, applied at the Skin step of
the Bear Hunter System.

## Construction procedure

The core build sequence (full detail with worked examples and the 2-4 Rule in
03-details/techniques/mindmap-construction.md):

1. Identify the biggest chunks — hypothetical at first; usually 3–7 for a single topic.
2. Arrange the chunks and their most important contained concepts so the relationship and flow
   between them is expressed.
3. Identify sub-concepts and sub-chunks and add them to the map, always relating each new item to
   the big picture.
4. Add detail until the topic is complete; information that is highly specific and unrelated to
   the rest of the map goes to memorisation techniques (e.g. flashcards) instead of the map.

General tips: leave big spaces between the first chunks so there is room for sub-chunks; make the
main flow between the largest chunks ("the backbone") visually very clear; relate each new item
to the big picture as it is added; constantly refine and revise the structure rather than
over-committing to the first attempt — "everything is just a hypothesis."

## GRINDE letters (VPReFRE's map-specific extension)

GRINDE maps represent the final and best version of a mind map; GRINDE is a mindmap-specific
extension of VPReFRE's guidelines, applied during the Skin step. Full per-letter criteria in
03-details/techniques/grinde-checklist.md; in compact form: **G**rouped (chunked per the 2-4
Rule), **R**eflective (layers of learning made explicit: backbone/logic → concepts → important
details), **I**nterconnected (arrows within and between layers, not radiating from one hub —
named failure: "wheel and spokes"), **N**on-verbal (doodles/visuals/personal shorthand, minimal
words), **D**irectional (a clear flow of influence tied to the big picture, dependent on
Aim-step adherence and logic-layer quality), **E**mphasised (layers visually distinguished by
size/colour/font).

## 2-4 Rule integration

The 2-4 Rule (full procedure and worked example in mindmap-construction.md) is the mechanical
rule Grouped enforces on the map: aim for two branches per node, never more than four (else
sub-chunk), never fewer than two (else merge/concatenate). It is the shared chunking mechanism
between the general importance-based-chunking concept and this map-specific construction
procedure.

## Completion-illusion caveat

A student (Live Clinic 20) reported a specific risk of treating a finished GRINDE map as
equivalent to mastery: "Whenever I am done with a mind map and document it, I feel like the
knowledge somehow shifts from my brain to the mind map, which makes this illusion that I 'know'
the material without actually retrieving it fully." The act of encoding gets conflated with the
act of transcription. The student's own proposed remedy — creating curveball questions
immediately after completing a map, as the next interleaved-retrieval step — is offered as a
hypothesis, not confirmed by an ICS response in captured text (the clinic answer is video-only,
DRM-locked).

## Map-navigation invention (student-contributed, not core-program-canonical)

A student working on a pathology differential-diagnosis map, unable to draw legible long
connecting lines between distant nodes, invented "'goto' points — similar to a 'choose your own
adventure book'" as a way to represent distant connections without illegible long lines across
the page (Live Clinic 20). This is presented as the student's own solution to a spatial-layout
problem on a large map; it is not confirmed, validated, or generalised by any captured ICS
response, and should be treated as a documented student contribution rather than a taught
technique.
