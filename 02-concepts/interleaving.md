## Canonical form (LC70): beyond scheduling

At its most sophisticated, interleaving is not primarily a spacing or topic-rotation schedule. Per
Live Clinic 70 ("Interleaving for complex problem solving and knowledge work"), interleaving
operates through **reframing, retrieval, and reconstruction of knowledge** — exploring the limits,
perspectives, and contexts of what is known, rather than only rotating which topic is being
practised next. The session explicitly frames this as correcting common misconceptions about
procedural fluency and about what interleaving "actually is" (illustrated in the source via a
football analogy).

A load-bearing corrective claim from this session: **many perceived procedural problems are
actually declarative gaps**. What looks like a failure to execute a skill can in fact be a failure
to comprehend the underlying concepts or relationships well enough to select a correct approach in
the first place.

### Comprehension vs. approach vs. execution: diagnosing the bottleneck first

Problem-solving breaks down into three components — **comprehension**, **approach**, and
**execution** — and diagnosing which of the three is the real bottleneck must precede choosing an
interleaving strategy. Approach (the pattern or system of thinking applied to a problem) is framed
as mattering more than execution in problem-solving. Interleaving, correctly targeted, is about
reconstructing knowledge to close the actual bottleneck — not about changing the problem itself or
simply doing more repetitions.

## Knowledge-type conditioning

**LC31 (interleaving across conceptual/procedural/conditional knowledge)**: interleaving technique
and level must be chosen with reference to which knowledge type — conceptual, procedural, or
conditional — is actually being tested, since these link together rather than existing as
independent silos. Documented claims from this session: identifying the gap in one's knowledge
matters more than the polish of a constructed answer sheet ("creating an answer sheet isn't as
important as identifying the gap"); interleaving techniques can be used to increase knowledge
transfer between domains via reasoning by analogy. A relationship between the choice of
interleaving technique/level and session duration is raised but its resolution is

**LC39 (learning procedural skills with interleaving)**: personalises interleaving technique
choice to how the knowledge is actually meant to be applied, built on a three-way knowledge-type
split (declarative, procedural, conditional — see knowledge-types). Documented claims:
**declarative knowledge must be acquired before procedural knowledge** (explicit sequencing

## Mechanism (memory dynamics)

Interleaving — mixing topics, forms, or conditions of practice rather than practising under fixed
conditions — strengthens discrimination and reconstruction because the brain learns better from
slight variation each time than from repetition under identical conditions (see revision-strategy).
Reviewing information from multiple angles and perspectives protects against curveball exam
questions and complex problem-solving failures, because the learner has already seen the
information presented and challenged in more than one way.

A common failure mode independent of sophistication level: many learners do not use a varied
interleaving repertoire, staying confined to only higher-order or only lower-order methods, or
omitting procedural elements when the subject requires them (see retrieval-practice; interleaving
table).
