## Camp-vs-Fundamentals revision relation

Revision strategy, together with Whole-Part-Whole (WPW) reteaching, is the Camp II (Growth phase)
upgrade that explicitly replaces two earlier, simpler treatments of revision:

- **Revision basics (Camp I)**: framed revision narrowly as the goal of achieving **true recall**
  — the ability to remember and apply information without cues — as distinct from weaker
  **recognition**-based revision or **cue**-dependent revision. This framing is correct as far as
  it goes but does not yet supply a strategy for finding or classifying gaps.
- **Test-target-teach (Fundamentals-2)**: an explicit interim/shortcut retrieval framework, offered
  so that progress does not halt while a learner is still mastering the full interleaving
  table. It substantially overlaps with the advanced group method and is explicitly substitutable
  with it once a learner is confident with that method (see
  03-details/techniques/test-target-teach.md for the full spec).

Camp II's revision strategy supersedes both by supplying a principled taxonomy of gap types and a
gap-driven method for selecting remedies, rather than a single generic retrieval routine. All other
Camp I techniques (flashcards, modified method of loci, Bear Hunter System with hipshot,
scheduling basics, focus training, PEER-Peer, OFF-Rest timing) continue unchanged alongside it.

## The four gap types (canonical taxonomy)

A knowledge gap is any missing information or relationship that is important and relevant,
identified by assessing and testing oneself. Four distinct gap types are distinguished, each with
its own diagnostic signal and its own remedy category:

1. **Declarative knowledge gap** — struggling to identify the correct approach or strategy to a
   problem. Common in engineering, coding, physics, and maths, where insufficient attention has
   gone to the conceptual network and excessive focus has gone to procedural repetition and
   practice instead.
2. **Retrieval gap** — struggling to recall information. Remedy: deeper relational encoding, or a
   lower-order memorisation method, depending on the type of information and the layer it belongs
   to. If a high volume of information is being forgotten, the underlying problem is almost always
   a suboptimal chunk structure — check the affected maps for waterfalling and spiderwebbing, and
   ensure chunks are intuitive; watch for "simple question and answer" patterns, which can
   undermine the inquiry process that should be surfacing relationships.
3. **Procedural knowledge gap** — struggling to consistently solve problems due to errors, caused
   by a lack of practice and execution-style interleaving. In some cases the underlying cause is
   actually a conceptual/declarative misunderstanding rather than a true procedural deficit.
4. **Higher-order knowledge gap** — struggling to apply knowledge in different contexts or more
   nuanced settings, caused by missing relationships rather than missing facts.

## Gap-driven revision selection

The correct remedy is selected by matching it to the diagnosed gap type above, rather than by
defaulting to one general-purpose revision method for every symptom. Key principles for finding
gaps:

- Look for gaps deliberately — do not avoid known weaknesses.
- Test at multiple orders of learning, not just the order that feels comfortable.
- Do not consult an answer sheet until a personal answer has been produced through self-study
  first; checking prematurely creates illusions of knowledge and is a common mistake.
- Struggling to answer a question indicates a gap even if the eventual answer is correct — a
  correct answer may simply be luck. **Difficulty matters more than correctness** as a gap signal.

Before revising, sweep quickly through course material to catch anything missed, and consider using
the modified method of loci to replace list-form information in a mind map with drawings (see
rote-and-mnemonics).

## Revision timing rules

**Expanding-gap spaced repetition**: the gaps between revision sessions on the same material should
get longer over time. Concretely, at least one or two retrieval sessions should occur within the
first 10 days of learning something, after which the gap between subsequent sessions should
continuously expand (see spacing for the canonical parameters this integrates with).

**Interleaving**: it is inefficient to always practise under the same conditions — the brain learns
better from slight variation in conditions each time (see interleaving).

**Gap-seeking**: actively look for gaps by reapplying the Aim step to the study material and to the
GRINDE map itself; parts of the map that are recognisably weak become targets for deliberately
constructed questions.

## End-of-week WPW slot and HVRP naming

Whole-Part-Whole reteaching is an instance of **high-volume retrieval practice (HVRP)** —
comprehensive retrieval that covers a topic's relationships and specific details together, in
contrast to brain dumps (the other named HVRP example), which are noted as less effective at
testing relationships and conceptual understanding specifically. HVRP is recommended as part of a
learner's spaced retrieval, scheduled for the **end-of-week or 2-week revision session** so that
subsequent, more targeted revision sessions can follow it. A mid-week HVRP session is added only
when weekly content volume is high; roughly 3–4 weeks later, and again shortly before a test or
exam, HVRP shifts to brain dump (linear or mind map) plus practice papers, with flashcards running
continuously via microlearning throughout rather than occupying their own dedicated session. See
03-details/techniques/wpw.md for the full HVRP implementation table.

## Test-target-teach as interim scaffold

Test-target-teach (Fundamentals-2) is preserved as the documented interim framework a learner uses
before the full interleaving table and revision strategy are mastered. It is explicitly
substitutable with the advanced group method for learners already confident with that method. Its
three parts — Test (apply knowledge using both self-made and premade questions), Target (identify
and start with areas of low confidence), Teach (teach for real, changing the order/approach rather
than simply explaining) — and its redefinition of a knowledge gap as anything struggled with,
answered incorrectly, *or* felt unconfident about, are kept in full at
03-details/techniques/test-target-teach.md.
