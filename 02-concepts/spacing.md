## Mechanism

Encoding and retrieval are two sides of the same learning coin, and are not fully separable
processes — the majority view in learning science treats them as fundamentally intertwined. The
corpus separates them only for practical/pedagogical reasons, because the physical actions and
techniques used to optimise each tend to be distinct.

The controlling relationship: as information is encoded to a higher quality, less retrieval is
required to maintain it — but the requirement never reaches zero, because the brain is never
leak-proof (there is always some baseline knowledge decay). Beyond inadequate encoding, "retrieval
failure" on its own is a common cause of decay of previously encoded information. Deficiencies in
encoding must be compensated with *more* retrieval in the short term, not less; as encoding
improves, the time required for retrieval naturally falls.

An ultra-strict spacing schedule is not necessary. Semi-regular, loosely-followed spacing that
keeps a learner on top of material is sufficient.

## Canonical parameters

- **Spacing intervals**: same-day, 1 day, 1 week, 1 month — followed loosely, not as a rigid
  calendar.
- **End-of-week revision retention target**: 80–90% of the material retained by the time of
  end-of-week revision (assuming effective encoding), meaning one week's material can be revised
  in just a few hours.
- **End-of-month (4-week) revision time estimate**: roughly half a day, because each subsequent
  interleaved retrieval session reduces further decay and re-encodes the information, compounding
  the saving.
- **Revisit cadence**: all material is revisited within 1 to 3 days of first learning it, then 1
  to 2 weeks later, then 2 to 4 weeks later.
- **Touches per month**: combined with prestudy and on-the-day studying, this cadence means any
  given piece of material is encoded and re-encoded (via interleaved retrieval) roughly 6 times in
  a month.

Combined with prestudying and batch learning ahead of a formal class/lecture schedule (concentrated
study bursts that get a learner a month or more ahead of "class," paired with just a few hours of
spaced retrieval per week), the total time cost and ease of maintaining this schedule is described
as extremely easy to sustain.

## Worked weekly example

| Day | Session type | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Monday and Tuesday | Same-day retrieval | New material learned and revised the same day. |
| Wednesday | One-day retrieval | Interleaved retrieval session for Monday + Tuesday content (and Wednesday too if new material was also learned that day). |
| Thursday | Same-day retrieval for new content | New material learned and revised the same day. |
| Friday or Saturday | One-week retrieval | Interleaved retrieval session covering all content over the last 2 weeks. |
| End of the month | One-month retrieval | Interleaved retrieval session for the last 4 weeks. |
| 3–4 weeks prior to assessment | One-month retrieval+ | Retrieve everything relevant for the assessment. |

This produces a same-day revision, a mid-week revision, an end-of-week revision (covering the
prior 2 weeks), and an end-of-month revision (covering the prior 4 weeks).

## Per-learner tuning rules

- **If spacing is too frequent**: unnecessary repetition and wasted work — the learner is being
  tested too early in the forgetting curve.
- **If spacing is too infrequent**: too much is forgotten by the time of revision, causing wasted
  time and risking an overwhelming retrieval session.
- **Expanding-gap spaced retrieval**: gradually increasing the interval between successive
  retrieval sessions on the same material. Research is inconclusive on whether expanding-gap
  spacing outperforms fixed-interval spacing, but it is still recommended because it makes workload
  significantly more manageable.
- **Low encoding skill**: the schedule will feel harder to sustain — an entire month's content can
  feel like a "titanic task" to revise. This is a symptom of encoding quality, not a flaw in the
  schedule itself; the fix is more retrieval in the short term while encoding skill develops, not
  an easier schedule.
- **High content volume**: an additional revision session can be inserted mid-month (fortnightly
  review) or mid-week (2-day review) if needed.
- **Spacing accuracy vs. quality**: the quality of interleaving matters more than the accuracy of
  spacing — a schedule can drift by days or even weeks and still work well, provided each session's
  quality remains high.
